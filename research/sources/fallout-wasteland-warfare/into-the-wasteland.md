INTRODUCTION
The Wasteland is alive and in constant flux. 
Creatures pass through, settlers come and go, 
items become buried while the winds blow away 
the dust revealing that which was once lost. Into 
the Wasteland allows you to take your force out 
into the Wasteland to explore and survive.
The ‘Wasteland Scenarios’ section describes how 
to create areas of the Wasteland which you can 
scavenge, travel, scout, neutralize, and more.
The inhabitants of the Wasteland do not stand 
there doing nothing, waiting for your arrival – they 
are hunting, traveling to new destinations, grazing, 
and more. The ‘Habitat’ section provides special 
AI rules to breathe life into the inhabitants of 
the Wasteland Scenarios, giving them their own 
purpose and natural behaviors.
Into the Wasteland also includes several 
Legendary creatures, and neutral creatures, 
discussed in their own section.
Finally, the ‘To the Rescue’ section is a solo/
co-op mini-campaign that takes you across 
several regions of the Wasteland in a desperate 
race to rescue kidnapped comrades utilizing the 
‘Wasteland Scenarios’ and ‘Habitat’ rules.
So, read on and prepare to head... Into the 
Wasteland.
INTO THE WASTELAND IS PRIMARILY AIMED AT PLAYERS 
PLAYING SOLO AND/OR CO-OPERATIVELY. HOWEVER, 
PLAYERS PLAYING VERSUS OTHER PLAYERS CAN ALSO USE 
THE WASTELAND SCENARIOS WITH INHABITANTS AS A 
SETTING FOR THEIR SCENARIOS.

FOWW ITW-001-111 ­— SECTION 2.1
WASTELAND SCENARIOS
The Wasteland may look scorched, dead and barren, 
but look closely and it is full with an ever-present, 
always-beating pulse of life which continues 
regardless of your presence. The littered remains 
of previous residents waiting to be discovered are 
merely the background to creatures migrating, 
hunting and nesting, Super Mutants setting up 
camp, Settlers gathering supplies, Wastelanders 
traveling, and more.
Wasteland Scenarios create areas of the Wasteland 
for players playing solo or cooperatively to explore 
by using a mixture of details, such as the size 
and shape of the battlefield, what might be found 
there, and so on. Players can use Wasteland 
Scenarios to go out into the world around 
them, whether to gain caps and items for their 
settlement, explore, pacify an area, or any other 
reason. But be warned, while the world is alive, 
many of its occupants often want you dead.

To create a Wasteland Scenario, follow the steps

1. PLAYER FORCE
The player creates their force of models up to

2. BATTLEFIELD SHAPE AND SIZE
Wasteland Scenario battlefields come in various 
shapes and sizes composed of multiple 1-foot by 
1-foot (Black by Black) sections. These sections 
are arranged orthogonally with any adjacent sides 
being fully aligned with each other. The Shape and 
Size determines the number of 1x1 sections as well 
as their layout, such as being 1x4, 2x3, an L-shape 
made of 4 sections, etc. Note that Battlefield edges 
block LoS and do not count as cover.
To determine the shape and size of the Wasteland 
Scenario battlefield, draw one of the Battlefield 
Shape and Size cards. The term Battlefield 
size (used later in these rules) refers to the total 
number of 1x1 sections the battlefield contains, i.e. 
a 2x2 battlefield has a size of 4, and this is shown 
in the top-right corner of the card.
Starting Locations
Each Battlefield shape has a fixed set of starting 
locations shown by the large yellow circles (

which are used by scenarios to set the starting 
positions for tokens. All starting locations are 
positioned half-way along the edges of the 1x1 
sections, which means they are Red from either end 
of that section’s side (see illustration on p.7).
Player Deployment Location
The large, gray square shows the Player 
Deployment location (explained on p.4). 
3. ENVIRONMENT
To determine the Environment, draw one of the 
Environment cards. The environment describes 
the type of area in which the scenario takes 
place. The environment gives advice on what may 
inhabit the area (see p.5), i.e. Mirelurks are more 
likely in coastal regions but not likely in areas 
of desert. Plus, the environment gives advice on 
what terrain may be encountered, and what Items 
might be found.

have the exact terrain. In all cases, be sure to 
include items that break up LoS and/or offer cover 
to give the Battlefield some tactical opportunities. 
Remember that lots of wide, open spaces gives 
an advantage to users of ranged weapons. Even 
the most desolate areas of the Wasteland have

Adding Inhabitants
Add a Counting Token to the center of each 
1x1 section. Some scenarios require additional 
Inhabitants; for example, some Battlefield Shape 
and Size cards and some Player Purpose cards add 
extra Inhabitants. For each additional Inhabitant to 
be placed, add 1 additional Counting Token to the 
center of a section, placing them in the sections with 
the fewest Inhabitant Counting Tokens, using those 
sections which are furthest from the player’s start 
location before those which are nearer.
For the half of the Counting Tokens (rounded up) 
which are nearest the player’s starting location, 
generate Inhabitants for each of these using the 
method described in ‘Generating Inhabitants’ 
section below. This will replace each of these 
tokens with the models of those Inhabitants on the 
battlefield and determine their Natural Behavior.
Unknown Inhabitants
The remaining Counting Tokens are Inhabitants 
whose details are not known yet – these are called 
‘Unknown Inhabitants’ which will be revealed 
during the game. Until a player model gets close 
enough, the shadows and sounds of the Wasteland 
make it too hard to pinpoint what, or even exactly 
where, these Unknown Inhabitants are.
At the end of each round, Scatter each of the 
Unknown Inhabitant tokens Yellow. If a token 
would be placed off the battlefield, place it on the 
battlefield nearest to the intended position.

check if it is real (see below). Similarly, if a player 
model (not Inhabitant model) ends any action or

The Wasteland Decks (Items, Events, Danger, 
Creature, and Stranger) can be created to reflect 
the scenario’s Environment. All environments are 
likely to have a mix of all types of Items, but the 
proportions of some types are likely to be different. 
To assist with this, each Environment card shows 
a series of icons for the different types of Items. A 
black icon indicates that type of Item is generally 
found in that environment, a red icon indicates that 
type of Item is much less likely to be found, and 
the green icon indicates that type of Item is much 
more likely to be found. These guides can be used to 
adjust the proportions of different Item types in the 
Item deck. Note that the Rifle icon 
 on this card 
means ‘Weapons of all types’, not just Rifle weapons.
The Danger, Creature and Stranger cards can 
also be tailored to match the environment. For 
example, if at a town with Feral Ghouls, include the 
Danger cards that include Feral Ghouls on them.
The number of cards you will need depends upon 
the amount of Searchables and Investigation 
Markers on the Battlefield. Add at least one Item for 
each Searchable, plus a few extras so there is some 
uncertainty what you may retrieve. If you need 
further cards for the Wasteland during a game, add 
additional cards from those you possess, or simply 
re-shuffle any discarded cards to save time.

HABITAT OVERVIEW
Walk into any area of the Wasteland and it is 
inhabited by Raiders traveling across it, Mole 
Rats aggressively guarding their nest, Deathclaws 
hunting prey. The Wasteland is alive with creatures, 
robots, humans, Super Mutants and many others 
all going about their business – they don’t just sit 
stationary until players come by. Habitat is a set of 
rules which allows these inhabitants to roam free, 
hunt, travel, etc.
So how does it work? Models found in the Wasteland 
are given one of several different Natural Behaviors 
such as Grazing, Hunting, Traveling, and so on – 
these models are called Inhabitants. A Natural 
Behavior is a special AI matrix which simply 
overrides an Inhabitant’s usual AI matrix. When 
an Inhabitant notices something that is relevant 
to their Natural Behavior, they ‘Exit’ their Natural 
Behavior temporarily and use their usual AI matrix 
with a specific objective. When the incident is over 
or resolved, the Inhabitant ‘Resumes’ their Natural 
Behavior AI matrix again.
When you want to explore an area of the Wasteland, 
you populate it with Inhabitants (and can use the 
Wasteland Scenario rules, (p.2), to create the entire 
area and scenario). Inhabitants can also be added 
as background to any scenario.

FRIENDLY MODELS FOR INHABITANTS
‘Friendly’ models for Inhabitants are models 
which are contextually on the same side. Models 
in the same Unit are always Friendly to each 
other, e.g. 2 Super Mutants using the same Unit 
card. A Friendly may also be a model of the same 
faction or aligned group even if in a different Unit, 
e.g. if the Inhabitants represent the Super Mutant 
forces in the area then all models with Super 
Mutant faction are friendlies to each other, as 
well as the Enslaved Tech who is working for the 
Super Mutants. (Note that models in the player(s) 
force are not Friendly to Inhabitants even if they 
are the same faction.) Units of the Creature type 
are not Friendly to each other, e.g. a Radscorpion 
is not friendly to a Mole Rat or vice versa, and one 
Unit of Mole Rats is not friendly to another Unit 
of Mole Rats. The same is true for Units of the 
Robot type.

LEGENDARY CREATURES
The Wasteland is a tough environment and the 
creatures in it are even tougher – but toughest 
of them all are the Legendary Creatures. If you 
think one of these legendary creatures is on your 
land, you’ve got it the wrong way around – you are 
on its land.
Legendary Units consist of multiple Unit cards 
which are used sequentially by name. The different 
cards for a Legendary Unit reflect its changing 
mood, approach, tactics and attacks. A player can 
include all, some, or just one of the Unit cards for a 
Legendary Unit in their force. If multiple cards for 
a Legendary Unit are included, only one Legendary 
Unit card is used for that Unit at a time and always 
the Legendary Unit card with the lowest number, 
i.e. use Deathskull Radscorpion I before Deathskull 
Radscorpion II. If playing with a single Legendary 
Unit card, it can be any card.
When a Legendary Unit model would be removed 
and the force contains a further Legendary Unit 
card for that model, do not remove the model. 
Instead, remove all its Damage tokens, and 
replace the Unit card with the next one. The 
cards are changed as soon as enough damage is 
accumulated, so any additional damage is carried 
over to the new card regardless of any change 
in armor value. When a card is changed, all 
conditions and tokens (except for damage) remain, 
any Items are added/removed as required, and any 
remaining Luck tokens are adjusted so the total 
matches the new card’s Luc value.
Be warned, Legendary Creatures are very 
dangerous – even if just facing a single card.

INTRODUCTION
Patrol duty. It has to be done, but sometimes, like 
this one, it can be so boring. Near home now, you see 
the reassuring smoke from the settlement’s cooking 
fires rising just over the ridge ahead. But the 
smoke is too dark. The columns are too numerous.

Many small fires burn, the contents of the buildings

They came without warning. Hit and run. Ghostly 
sightings in the Wasteland, farmers going missing, 
creatures on the move outside of normal migration 
patterns – they all seemed unconnected but now it’s 
clear – the Institute are active in this region.
Several settlers have been kidnapped. You can’t 
let your settlement’s sacking and pillaging go 
unanswered, and you can’t leave those taken in 
enemy hands. Without waiting, your patrol heads 
out along the attackers’ trail. The sooner you find 
them, the less they’ll be prepared for your rescue 
attempt, yet the faster you move, the less gear you 
can gather as you go. How will you balance urgency 
versus preparedness? Will you reach them in time?
Welcome to this solo/co-op mini-campaign which 
takes you on the trail of the Institute raiding party 
through areas of the Wasteland by playing a series 
of Wasteland Scenarios and using the Habitat rules

You will travel across a section of the Wasteland

zone, you will play a scenario. At some points, you

matching numbered section (taking care not to 
accidentally read any other sections and spoil any 
surprises later), and follow the instructions.
