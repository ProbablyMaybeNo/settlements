INTRODUCTION
In the Wasteland, your Settlement is your refuge, 
your home and the key to your survival. Homestead
expands the Settlement experience in Fallout: 
Wasteland Warfare in several ways. Settlement 
Events can now occur during Settlement Use – will 
you pay off the Raider gang by giving them some 
items to go away (knowing they’ll be back for more) 
or try to persuade them to leave for good, knowing it 
could turn ugly? Will you spend time and resources 
preparing your Settlement against a coming storm, or 
will you simply hope it changes direction? 
Using Homestead, your Settlement becomes a 
physical entity requiring you to draw the layout 
of where each Structure is placed as you build. 
Plus, your Settlement can become damaged, taking 
Structures out of action and requiring resources to 
make repairs.
As a key part of your future in the Wasteland, your 
Settlement will almost certainly be the focus of 
hostile intent from others who want to dislodge 
you from the region, take your items, or just cause 
mayhem. Homestead allows you to resolve these 
attacks instantly or play out the scenario of the attack 
with your actual Settlement layout as the battleﬁeld.
For players looking for a tougher challenge, Survival 
Mode allows you to have a persistent roster of models 
in your Settlement. Damage from one battle will 
carry over to the next or need to be treated by food or 
Stimpaks, or even new Structures such as the Medical 
Center.
So, grab your shovel, break new ground, and prepare 
to build and defend your Homestead...
RESOURCES
Homestead introduces a new currency to Settlement 
Mode called ‘Resources’. Resources represent critical 
components used for building and maintaining the 
settlement and anything within it, such as wood, 
glass, aluminum, screws, and rubber.
Resources are required to repair damage to structures, 
reinforce structures, extend structures, and other 
tasks. In addition, they are required for some 
Settlement Events and Consequences, explore cards, 
and some scenarios too.
Resources can be gained from some new Structures 
which can break Items down into Resources or provide 
new Resources. Resources can also be gained from 
explore cards and Settlement Events.
While using their Settlement, a player keeps a 
running total of their Resources (similar to their 
running total of Caps). If a player needs to spend 
Resources, they deduct the amount required from 
their total (which can never go below zero).
Unlike Caps, Resources can only be kept between 
each Settlement Use if there is appropriate storage 
for them in a new type of Structure called Resource 
Sheds. At the end of using their Settlement, a player 
must discard any Resources in excess of the maximum 
Resources which can be held by their Resource Sheds.
HOMESTEAD CAN BE USED BY BOTH PLAYERS PLAYING 
AGAINST OTHER PLAYERS, AND PLAYERS PLAYING SOLO 
AND/OR CO-OPERATIVELY. TO USE HOMESTEAD, A PLAYER 
MUST BE USING THE SETTLEMENT RULES INCLUDED IN THE 
CAMPAIGN HANDBOOK.

STRUCTURE SIZES
Each Structure has a size which is the minimum 
number of squares it must occupy on the Settlement 
Layout (which is a diagram of the Settlement, 
described on p.8).
Small Structures
Some Structures are classed as ‘Small Structures’. 
These are small enough that they can ﬁt inside some 
other Structures. Small Structures are:

All Crafting Structures (except Creature Pen)

All Boost Structures

All Infrastructure Structures (except Crop Fields)

Lockers

Turrets
TOTAL STRUCTURES
Unlike the regular Settlement rules, the total number 
of Structures is not used in Homestead; instead, 
physical geographic area in which to place Structures 
is the limitation, and buying more Land increases the 
size of that area (see Settlement Layout, p.8). Unless 
otherwise stated, the total amount of each different 
structure that can be built is unlimited.
NEW STRUCTURES
Homestead adds new Structures to the game. 
Some allow the collection, creation, and storage of 
Resources, while others help remove injuries, add 
defenses, and provide buildings for Small Structures 
to be housed within. As with existing Structures, 
unless indicated, each Structure requires 1 power to 
function during Step 2 of the Settlement process.

DEFENSE STRUCTURES
Defense Structures may be built during Step 2 in 
addition to during Step 1. As soon as an effect on the 
Settlement must be resolved, Defense Structures 
may not be built until that effect is resolved. For 
example, if a Settlement Event card states ‘Allocate 4 
Structural Damage’ or ‘Resolve a Settlement Attack 
with Attack Rating 5’, no new Defense Structures can 
be built before that effect is resolved.
POWER AND WATER DURING STEP 2
If power is reduced during Step 2 and there is not 
enough to supply all the undamaged Structures, the 
player must immediately choose which Structures lose 
power until there is enough power to supply all the 
undamaged Structures. The same applies for water.
If power is increased during Step 2 when there was 
not enough to supply all the undamaged Structures, 
the player must immediately choose the unpowered, 
undamaged Structure(s) that the power increase is 
routed to. The same applies for water.
Losing or gaining power or water does not change 
whether a Structure has been used or not during this 
Settlement Use.
STARTING SETTLEMENT
A newly built Settlement includes the Structures 
below for free. (Note that this is slightly different from 
the Starting Settlement in the existing Settlement 
rules.)
1 x Land
Beneﬁt: Can place Structures within 
an 18x18 area.
2 x Small Generators
Beneﬁt: Provides 6 Power.
1 x Stores
Beneﬁt: Take 1 item card or up to 2 
Boost cards into battle.
1 x Maintenance Shed
Beneﬁt: Retain 1 card after battle.
1 x Listening Post
Beneﬁt: Ability to Explore (see 
Campaign Handbook, p.22).
1 x Resource Stand
Beneﬁt: Can buy Resources.
1 x Hut
Beneﬁt: May contain up to 1 Small 
Structure.
Any Caps earned during battles up to the start of 
building a new Settlement are gone – they have been 
used to buy the starting Structures and establish 
the new Settlement. Therefore, when starting a new 
Settlement, the player starts with zero Caps. 
USING AN EXISTING SETTLEMENT WITH HOMESTEAD
Players can use Homestead with an existing 
Settlement built prior to using Homestead. If any of 
the starting Structures (above) are not present, those 
missing Structures are immediately added for free 
to their existing Settlement the ﬁrst time they use it 
with Homestead.
The player must draw their Settlement Layout 
(described in the next section) before adding any 
new Structures. If a player has bought additional 
Land already, expand the area to be built upon by 
the appropriate amount before starting to draw the 
Settlement Layout. If all the Structures already 
owned cannot ﬁt onto the Land they own, the player 
can scrap Structures to gain Caps with which to 
purchase more Land, or they can leave off any 
Structures that will not ﬁt until such a time that they 
buy Land so they can ﬁt – when not drawn on the 
Settlement Layout, those Structures are not used or 
considered part of the Settlement.

SETTLEMENT DAMAGE
Homestead adds the possibility of Settlements 
being attacked and, as a result, Structures may 
suffer damage. Some cards or scenarios may cause 
damage to the Structures in the player’s Settlement. 
In addition, if a Settlement is part of a Settlement 
scenario, Structures can be attacked by models. 
STRUCTURE DAMAGE STATUS
Each Structure is either undamaged, damaged, badly 
damaged, or wrecked. When an undamaged Structure 
suffers Structural Damage, it becomes damaged. 
When a damaged Structure suffers Structural 
Damage, it becomes badly damaged. When a badly 
damaged structure suffers Structural Damage, it 
becomes wrecked.
When a Structure with Small Structures inside 
suffers Structural Damage, the Small Structures 
within may also suffer Structural Damage. When 
the Structural Damage occurs, roll the

Small Structure inside: If any

Reinforced Structures
If a Structure is Reinforced and is to suffer Structural 
Damage, roll the

Structure suffers the Structural Damage; if the result 
is 3 or 4, the Structural Damage does not affect the 
Structure and is ignored.
DAMAGED AND BADLY DAMAGED STRUCTURES
When a Structure is damaged or badly damaged, 
its ability is unavailable until it is repaired to being 
undamaged. Any Small Structures inside damaged 
and badly damaged Structures can still be used as 
normal if they are undamaged.
WRECKED STRUCTURES
When a Structure is wrecked, it remains in the 
Settlement and its ability is unavailable. Wrecked 
Structures cannot be repaired. Wrecked Structures 
can only be removed by scrapping them. Any Small 
Structures inside wrecked Structures cannot be used 
even if they are undamaged, but they can be moved 
using the normal rules.
Walkways, Ramps, Staircases, and Ladders
When a Structure becomes wrecked, any Walkways, 
Ramps, Staircases, or Ladders connected to that 
Structure are removed. Also, any Walkways, Ramps, 
Staircases, and Ladders that are no longer supported 
because a Structure required for their placement has 
been removed are themselves removed, i.e. a walkway 
supported at one end purely by a ladder would be 
removed if that ladder is removed. When a Walkway, 
Ramp, Staircase, or Ladder is removed, any models on 
them fall vertically downwards, then apply the rules 
for falling.
REINFORCING STRUCTURES
Reinforced Structures are less likely to suffer 
Structural Damage (described above) and take less 
effort to defend (see Settlement Attacks, p.15). An 
undamaged structure can be Reinforced during any 
Step of Settlement Use by spending 2 Resources. 
Structures which are already Reinforced cannot 
be Reinforced again. Small Structures cannot be 
Reinforced. A Structure loses its Reinforced status 
if it stops being undamaged. When a Structure is 
Reinforced, an ‘R’ is written inside the shape. The ‘R’ is 
removed if it loses its Reinforced status.
RECORDING DAMAGE ON THE SETTLEMENT LAYOUT
When a Structure is Damaged, Badly Damaged, or 
Wrecked, write a ‘D’, ‘BD’ or ‘W’ inside its shape on the 
Settlement Layout. Each Structure can only have one 
of these letters at a time. If undamaged, no letter is 
required. (Remember to remove a Structure’s ‘R’ if it is 
Reinforced and stops being undamaged.)
REPAIRING A STRUCTURE
A structure can be repaired during any Step of 
Settlement Use by spending 2 Resources. Repairing a 
badly damaged Structure returns it to being damaged. 
Repairing a damaged Structure returns it to being 
undamaged. A wrecked Structure cannot be repaired. 
Repairing a Structure does not change whether the 
Structure has been used or not during this Settlement 
Use.

SETTLEMENT ATTACKS
If a Settlement Attack is declared by a card (during 
Step 2 of Settlement), or called for by a scenario, a 
player can resolve the attack by either using the 
instant method based on the Settlement’s values, or 
by playing out a Settlement Scenario (see Settlement 
Scenarios, p.16).
An attack has an Attack Rating – the higher the 
number, the larger and more serious the attack. Some 
attacks can require rolling dice to determine the ﬁnal 
value of the Attack Rating value, and some can alter 
the Settlement before the attack is calculated, i.e. 
power may be interrupted just before the attack.
RESOLVING A SETTLEMENT ATTACK BY PLAYING A SETTLEMENT SCENARIO
An attack on a Settlement can be resolved by playing 
a Settlement Scenario – these are scenarios where the 
player’s Settlement is laid out on the battleﬁeld and 
the scenario of the attack is played there. Structures 
can be attacked by models, items may get stolen, and 
so on. Settlement Scenarios can be played against 
another player (playing the attackers) or by the AI. 
The Attack Rating is used during set-up, and other 
details may be speciﬁed by the attack too. See the 
Settlement Scenarios section (p.16) for details.
RESOLVING A SETTLEMENT ATTACK BY USING THE INSTANT METHOD
The instant method allows an attack to be resolved 
without playing it as a scenario. When a Settlement 
Attack is to be resolved using the instant method, ﬁrst 
calculate the Settlement’s Defense Rating by starting 
with a Defense Rating of 3 and then:
1.
For every Guard Post with power:
a.
Add 1 if there is a Barricade along 1 of its 
sides.
b.
Add 2 if there is a Barricade along 2 or 3 of its 
sides.
2.
For every Watch Tower with power, add 1.
3.
For every Turret with power:
a.
For Machine Gun Turrets: Add an amount 
equal to its Mk, i.e. Mk III would add 3.
b.
Add 1 for every Laser Turret.
c.
Add 3 for every Heavy Laser Turret.
4.
Deduct 1 for every 4 Structures in the Settlement 
(excluding any Small Structures inside other 
Structures and any Reinforced Structures).
Do not include:

Any Defense Structures that require power but do 
not have power, or

Any Defense Structures which are not undamaged.
A Settlement Defense Rating has a minimum of 0.
For example, a Settlement has 1 Machine Gun 
Turret Mk III and 1 Guard Post with 1 barricade 
along its sides. The Settlement has 13 Structures 
but 2 of those are small Structures inside 
another Structure and 1 is Reinforced, so only 10 
Structures are considered which means 2 will be 
deducted. Therefore, the total Defense Rating is 3 
(starting value) + 3 (Turret) + 1 (Guard Post) - 2 
(1 for every 4 Structures being considered) = 7 - 2 
= 5.
Determining Structural Damage
When an attack occurs, the Settlement suffers 
Structural Damage equal to the amount the Attack 
Rating is greater than the Defense Rating. If the 
Defense Rating is equal to or higher than the Attack 
Rating, the attack does no Structural Damage.
Continuing the previous example, an attack occurs 
on the Settlement with an Attack Rating of 8. The 
Settlement’s Defense Rating is 5, so 3 Structural 
Damage is dealt and allocated one at a time.
Allocating Structural Damage
Any Structural Damage caused is allocated one at a 
time as described in the earlier Allocating Structural 
Damage section, p.14.

SETTLEMENT SCENARIOS
Settlement Scenarios are played like regular 
scenarios but the terrain on the battleﬁeld matches 
the Settlement’s Layout. The player defends their 
Settlement against the attack with the Settlement as 
the battleﬁeld. The attacker in Settlement Scenarios

player is playing the attacker, they may use their own 
force, or simply facilitate the attack using a different 
force, i.e. they have their own settlement of Survivors 
but are playing out an attack by Raiders.
Usually Settlement Scenarios are played when 
something during Settlement Use, or a campaign 
outcome, initiates an attack on the Settlement; 
however, players can play a Settlement Scenario at 
any time if they wish.
ATTACK DETAILS
If any details about the attack are speciﬁed by 
whatever initiates the attack – usually a card 
or campaign outcome – override any random 
determination mentioned in this section with the 
details on the card; for example, a card initiating a 
Settlement Attack states it is by Raiders who deploy 
using the Surprise method, but their goal is unknown 
so the goal would still be randomized (as described 
below).

SURVIVAL MODE
Survival Mode is an option for players looking for a 
tougher challenge, primarily by adding a permanent 
roster of personnel in your Settlement. Damage from 
one battle is carried over to the next and removed 
models may not be available for one or more battles. 
FORCE ROSTER
For Survival Mode, a player must keep a list of all the 
individual models in their Force; this is done using the 
Roster. (While recorded individually, models can still 
be grouped in multiple-model Units when used in a 
battle.)
Starting Survival Mode
When a player starts using Survival Mode, they pick 
the Force they want to use and, just like in normal 
Settlement Use, the Force’s cost does not need to be 
paid out of the Settlement’s total Caps. Each of the 
models in the Force is written individually onto an 
empty Roster.
Hiring New Models
After the ﬁrst Force is created, any new model 
to be added to the Roster must be ‘hired’ which 
requires paying that Unit’s Caps cost paid out of the 
Settlement’s total Caps (in the same way buying a 
Structure or Perk costs Caps). When a new model is 
hired, it is added to the Roster.
Creating a Force for Battle
When creating a Force, the player is limited to the 
models on their Roster, i.e. if a player has 2 Scavvers 
on their Roster, then they can ﬁeld up to 2 Scavvers in 
their Force, but would need to hire more (paying the 
Caps cost for each out of the Settlement’s total Caps) 
if they want more in their Force for a Battle. 
Models whose Fate box shows Delayed, Lost, or 
Captured (described later) are not available for Battle, 
as they are not currently in the Settlement and cannot 
be included in a Force.
Heroic
Models can be Heroic for a battle if the ‘Battles’ box 
for that model on the roster shows 2 or more. The 
cost for being Heroic is part of their model cost when 
creating a Force as usual (and is not paid out of the 
Settlement’s total Caps). A model does not have to 
be Heroic even if they qualify to be so or even if they 
were Heroic in a previous Battle.
