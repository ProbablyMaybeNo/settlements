The World stands on the brink of a potentially transformative conflict.
The United States grapples with internal challenges and external threats, while projecting power across the 
globe, along with their traditional allies.
In the East, the ambitious Chinese regime, fueled by a vision of reshaping the global order, makes calculated 
moves to expand its influence. From the contested waters of the South China Sea to the political manoeuvrings 
in Taiwan, China emerges as a formidable player, challenging the established world order with determination 
and guile.
Meanwhile, a resurgent Russia, emboldened by perceived historical slights, pursues a dual strategy. In Eastern 
Europe, it destabilised the fragile equilibrium, challenging the established norms of territorial integrity. 
Simultaneously, Russia extends its reach into Africa, leveraging political instability to enhance its geopolitical 
foothold.
In the complex and ever-evolving landscape of the Middle East, Iran seeks to bolster its military capabilities to 
defend its interests and project power in the region. This includes developing advanced technologies, enhancing 
its cyber capabilities, and ensuring a robust defence posture against external threats.
Iran’s influence extends through proxy networks and non-state actors, enabling the nation to shape events in 
neighbouring countries. This strategic approach allows Tehran to pursue its interests while maintaining a degree 
of plausible deniability, complicating the international response to its actions. With a rich history and a strategic 
position in the heart of the region, Iran’s aspirations are intricately tied to its regional influence, historical 
grievances, and the broader dynamics of global power struggles.
Nations become entangled in a web of deception, cyber warfare, and covert operations, setting the stage for a 
high-stakes confrontation that threatens to plunge the world into chaos.
The consequences of unchecked ambitions and power plays among these major players have far-reaching 
implications, not only for the nations directly involved but for the stability and security of the entire world.

Spectre Operations provides the comprehensive rules for wargames set in the modern era on the tabletop. These rules 
will allow you to play a myriad of the situations that define Modern Warfare. Lead an Operation consisting of Combined 
forces against a Near Peer Opponent, Conduct a Special Operations mission to Eliminate a High Value Target, or Covertly 
Infiltrate and conduct an Intelligence gathering mission against a sensitive target - Spectre Operations allows you to 
play all aspects of these missions, and more.
Realism is the heart of Spectre Operations. The rules and accompanying range of miniatures from Spectre Miniatures 
have been developed over a decade of research and advice from experienced veterans of numerous Armed Forces and 
Law Enforcement.
This book is dedicated to those that protect us overseas and at home, and to those that have paid the ultimate price to 
do so.

GETTING STARTED
WHAT YOU NEED TO PLAY:
DICE
Spectre Operations uses a 10 sided dice, called a D10. You will need several of 
these and it is a good idea to have several in different colours. 
TAPE MEASURE
You’ll need a tape measure marked in inches to play.
MINIATURES 
You’ll need miniatures to play. Spectre Miniatures sell an expansive range of miniatures that are designed 
to be used in games of Spectre Operations. These rules work best with 28mm models, which will need to 
accurately represent the force you are playing. Weapon distinction is an important part of the game, so it 
is vital that you and your opponent are clear on what model is equipped with what.
PLAY SURFACE 
You will need a defined table size that the game will take place on. This is usually a minimum of 2’x2’, with 
most games on a 3’x3’ or 4’x4’ - but there is no upward limit for larger games using several Elements and/
or multiple players. Small games will have 1 or 2 Elements on each side.
TERRAIN
Often a scenario will advise on the terrain to be used, but in general it is important to have enough terrain 
to be able to cover at least half of the play surface. Cover and scenery that obscures Elements will aid 
in their Survivability and increase Situational Awareness, so it is a good idea to provide areas of dense 
cover and some open areas. Some games may take place in a single building, 
such as a bank, with a 
surrounding parking lot or adjacent buildings. 
You can get inspiration for setting up your table from movies, video 
games, and the world around you! It can be a fun exercise to study 
your local town, industrial estates, transport hubs or isolated buildings 
and how the areas around them look.
ELEMENT CARDS/PEN AND PAPER
It can be helpful to fill out Element cards, with the info you 
will need to access regularly in a game, as well as a place 
to track an Element’s stress using a dice. These are available 
from Spectre Miniatures.  You can also keep track of this with 
a pen and paper!

TESTS
Tests are performed regularly in Spectre Operations. These rolls 
are made using a D10, which is numbered 1-10. These will be 
dice rolls made, needing a score equal or greater than a specified 
number, such as Ballistics or Stress. The number rolled can be 
modified by certain in-game factors, such as the distance a 
weapon is used in an engagement, or any cover an Element 
may be in. Regardless of modifiers (unless explicitly stated), a 
1 is always a fail and a 10 is always a pass. Sometimes luck or 
chance are on your side.

LINE OF SIGHT
Spectre Operations uses True Line of Sight (LoS). True Line of 
Sight is interpreting what a miniature would be able to see from 
their point of view. Sometimes this will be very clear, but other 
times may require you to try and get your eye level as close to 
the model as possible to determine what they would be able to 
see. LoS is very important for determining which Elements can 
be targeted or seen by other Elements.
FACING AND FIELD OF FIRE
Models and Elements always have a defined facing, which 
effects their Field of Fire (FoF). The Facing of a model is usually 
defined as the direction the head of the miniature is looking 
towards. This may not always be the case and if in doubt, 
tell your opponent the direction the model is Facing. Some 
players find it useful to mark the Facing of the Miniature on the 
base, with a Line or Dot. The Field of Fire of all Elements is 90º, 
although some specialist equipment may restrict or improve 
this. FoF and Facing are always in the same direction. 
A Miniature may change the Facing/FoF at the start or finish of 
an Engagement Action.
It is important to pay attention to the FoF of your fighters, as 
Engagements can be affected by this when making Situational 
Awareness Tests. Some Equipment may also benefit from 
particular Facings or FoF.
MEASURING
Distances are always measured from and to the centre of a 
model. When Engaging an Element that has models in two 
different Engagement ranges when making an Engagement 
Action, use the greater distance.

FORCES
Each Player controls a Force, made up of individual Elements. This Force can be selected to fit a narrative, or built 
using the Force Builders in this book (page 99). Forces will be defined as Trained or Untrained, which are both 
further defined by their Fighter Profiles. 
Each Force type will have a particular style of playing: for instance a Tier 1 Force will usually be small in size, with 
1 or 2 extremely well equipped and trained Elements, whereas a Militia Force can have numerous Elements, with 
a variety of weapons and will often include vehicles. Scenarios will usually provide a limit for how many resources 
each player can dedicate to the Operation. Resources work in the same way as Points in many other Wargames.
ELEMENTS
Elements in Spectre Operations represents 1 or more fighters and/
or a vehicle that work together as a team when on the Battlefield. 
This group could be a Lone Operator, Police Partners, a Street Gang, 
team of Militia or a Fireteam of Professional Soldiers.
Each force will be composed of 1 or more Elements. These 
Elements could be a Squad of 4-8 Trained Soldiers, a Sniper Team 
of two, a large Militia gang of 12-20 fighters or a single vehicle.
The main focus of the rules is intended to be on the squad level, 
with one or more Squads of 4-8 fighters, but the rules work very 
well scaled down. For example, a single model can represent an 
element for more specialist games, such as those using Covert 
Action Rules.
Games can also be scaled up for much larger engagements, using 
several Elements, including Vehicles. These games should take 
place on a larger table size and will usually take more planning 
and time to complete the mission.

COHESION DISTANCES
Keeping your elements in coherent spacing is vital to your elements performing effectively. Members of an element 
that become stranded from their teammates will find it difficult to perform actions and fire effectively. The better 
trained and more experienced an element is, the longer the distance at which they can perform. Certain Equipment can 
increase this distance, or remove or lessen any negative modifiers for being out of cohesion.
Models in an Element must stay a number of Inches equal to or under their Cohesion. For example, a Militia Element is 
Untrained, so must deploy and move so that the models within the Element remain at a maximum of 3” from another 
model in that Element. In a Trained Element, this distance is 5” from another model.
While in Cohesion, the Element performs normally and all models within the Element perform the same actions (where 
appropriate). 
If any models are out of cohesion, they cannot perform the same action as their Element, and the Entire Element must 
use the Regroup Action to move into unit cohesion or towards their element if reaching cohesion is not possible.
Every model out of Cohesion raises the Element’s Stress Value (page 31) by 1 for every turn they are out of Cohesion.
SITUATIONAL AWARENESS.
The miniatures in an Element are used to represent real people and the way they would behave in the real world. The 
static miniatures are in essence tokens that could represent a real fighter that is moving around his location, ducking in 
and around cover, and interacting with the Environment. 
Situational Awareness is used to represent fighters’ instincts and training to use their Environment to their advantage 
to, at its base level, avoid being shot. This acts in the same way as a ‘save’ in other traditional wargames, with various 
ways that the test can be improved, as detailed on page 29.

Spectre Operations games are divided into turns, and each turn consists of two Action Phases. During each Action 
Phase, one player uses their Elements to carry out their desired Actions. The first player, typically designated as the 
Attacker, completes their Action Phase before the second player begins theirs. Players can also React to enemy Actions, 
interrupting the opposing player’s Action Phase. However, Reacting will limit the number of Actions available during 
the player’s own turn. The game turn ends after both players have finished their Action Phases.
ORDER OF PLAY
1: PLAYER 1 ACTION PHASE
2: PLAYER 2 ACTION PHASE
3: TURN END

Momentum is a vital aspect of Spectre Operations. Momentum must 
be maintained to fully utilise your force to complete your objective 
as quickly as possible. Taking too long to achieve your objective will 
have a negative effect on your momentum and increases the risk of 
the situation escalating. Managing Momentum is key to a successful 
mission.
Momentum is generated by each Element at the start of every 
game turn. This means that Elements that use the Rally Action to 
remove Suppressed and/or Pinned Status will not generate their full 
Momentum until the following turn.
Any Momentum Unspent at the end of the turn is lost.
Your Force gains 2 Momentum points for each Element in your Force. 
All Momentum generated by your Elements goes into a pool of 
Momentum for your Force.
During turns 1-3, an additional +1 Momentum is also generated.
Elements within your Force can then spend this Momentum on 
Actions during your turn and Reactions during the enemy’s turn.
IMPORTANT: Any unspent Momentum is lost at the end of a game 
turn and cannot be carried over
Additional Momentum Modifiers
+1 Momentum during the first three turns (1-3)
-1 Momentum for each Suppressed Element
-2 Momentum for each Pinned Element
REMEMBER:
Even with negative modifiers, a Force always generates a minimum 
of 1 Momentum each turn.
Elements that use the Rally Action to remove Suppressed or Pinned 
status only generate their full Momentum from the following turn.
A Force will never Generate less than 1 Momentum each turn,

USING MOMENTUM
Momentum may be spent by elements to perform actions. Each Element in your force may be selected to 
perform actions in any order, but may only be selected to perform actions once per turn (excluding reactions). 
You do not need to assign Momentum to Elements before selecting them to perform Actions. You may keep 
performing Actions with an Element until you decide to select another Element to perform Actions with. This 
allows you to make decisions ‘on the move’ depending on the success or failure of the Actions performed.
There is no limit to how many actions each element can perform, other than being limited by how much 
momentum your force has. Every action can be performed more than once by each element, unless stated 
by the action or another factor. Some weapons, for example, may restrict movement, or be unable to make an 
Engagement action if a manoeuvre action is performed. Each action costs a single point of Momentum, but 
spending additional Momentum to perform the action will often give benefits to the Action, usually in the form 
of adding extra Dice to tests.
Each individual Action may not be performed in repetition; for example, an Element cannot make an 
Engagement action, then another Engagement action, they must use another Action in 
between.
You do not need to spend all of your Momentum, it may be wise to reserve 
some to use as reactions to Enemy Actions.

STEPS TO FOLLOW WHEN MAKING AN ENGAGEMENT ACTION
In Spectre Operations, an Engagement action allows an 
Element to attack a Target Element using ranged weapons. 
This requires Line of Sight (LoS) and the target to be within 
the attacking Element's Field of Fire (FoF).
The Element performing the attack is the Engaging 
Element, and individual models within it making the 
attack are Shooters.
DURING AN ENGAGEMENT ACTION:
	
►
Every model in the Engaging Element meeting the 
LoS and FoF criteria can participate as a Shooter.
	
►
You have the option to include only some models 
from the Engaging Element in the shooting.
KEY DEFINITIONS:
	
►
Target Element: The Element being attacked by 
ranged weapons in an Engagement Action.
	
►
Line of Sight (LoS): An unobstructed visual 
connection between the Shooter and the Target 
Element.
	
►
Field of Fire (FoF): The area within which an Element can effectively engage targets.
	
►
Shooter: Each individual model within the Engaging Element that makes an attack.
	
►
Engaging Element: The Element initiating the Engagement Action.
To make an engagement action, measure the distance from the shooter to the furthest visible model in the Target 
Element. The distance measured will dictate the range modifiers for the weapons they are using. Depending on whether 
the target is within CQB, Engagement or Extreme Range of the weapon used, it may add or subtract to the modifier to 
hit.
These modifiers will either add to, or subtract from, the shooter’s Ballistics test. To determine if a shot is accurate, roll a 
D10 for each model taking part in the Action and compare it to the shooter's ballistics value, after modifiers have been 
applied to the dice roll. Any dice roll that equals or exceeds the Shooters Ballistics Value is an Accurate Shot. Any dice that 
are lower Miss the Target Element and have no effect.
It is common for an Element to have more than 1 weapon type carried by each fighter. It is important to measure each 
of these weapons separately to determine the range modifiers for each weapon. We suggest using different coloured 
dice to represent these so all of the dice can be rolled together, or if a weapon or weapons have multiple special rules, roll 
them separately.

SITUATIONAL AWARENESS
CLOSE QUARTERS BATTLE (CQB)
Close Quarters Battle (CQB), also known as Close Quarters Combat (CQC), occurs when opponents engage at very close 
range. This typically happens in urban areas and buildings, but can also occur in confined spaces like ships, airplanes, 
tunnels, and dense forests. CQB is challenging and dangerous due to the close proximity of combatants, increasing 
the likelihood of successful attacks.
CQB IN SPECTRE OPERATIONS
In Spectre Operations, CQB range is within 6 inches, unless stated otherwise. Elements within this range are 
considered in CQB for actions like engagements. Due to the difficulty of CQB, elements must test to hit using their 
current Stress Level, rather than Ballistics. Modifiers still apply.
STRESS MANAGEMENT IN CQB
Managing stress, or applying stress to opponents before engagement, is crucial in CQB. Even untrained individuals can 
land lethal hits at close range, highlighting the importance of stress management.
MODIFIERS FOR ENGAGEMENT:
	
►
+/-X to hit depending on the range of the weapon used.
	
►
+1 dice for each model per additional  Momentum spent on the Action.
	
►
-1 to hit per additional Momentum spent on the Action.
	
►
+1 to hit if the Engagement Action is being made against the same Target Element that was 
Engaged in the previous turn.
	
►
+1 to hit for every full 4” the Engaging Element is higher in elevation above the Target Element.
	
►
-1 to hit for every point of Momentum spent on Manoeuvring, if the Engaging Element made a 
Manoeuvre Action before the Engagement Action.
The ability to identify incoming fire, its accuracy, and direction depends on training and experience. Reaction to 
incoming fire and the ability to find cover or otherwise minimize your risk of being hit during an Engagement is 
represented by a Situational Awareness Test.
For each accurate shot from any Engagement, the target Element may make an awareness check. If the test is 
successful, the target was able to react successfully to incoming and accurate fire and has not become a potential 
Lethality. However, for each successful Awareness check, the Element will increase its stress value by 1.
	
►
-2 Awareness if the Element is being shot from outside of their FoF.
	
►
-1 Awareness if the Engaging Element is using entirely Suppressed Weapons.

ASSAULT ACTION
ASSAULT ACTION
1 Momentum: 1 Move then 1 dice per model in Assault.
Each Additional momentum: 1 additional dice per model in Assault.
Combat typically involves swift, brutal attacks against an adversary. Although not common on the modern 
battlefield, fighting in close quarters can result in hand to hand combat.
INITIATING AN ASSAULT
To start an Assault Action, the Attacking Element can perform a Manoeuver Action up to 3”, as long as this movement 
allows at least one model from the Attacking Element to reach a model in the Target Element.
Remember: A Snap Shot Reaction can be used against this Manoeuver Action.
An Assault action can be performed by any model in base contact with a model in an opposing Element. All Models 
are able to make an attack using the Unarmed combat weapon profile, although they may be equipped with a 
Combat Weapon. 
Note: Some weapons may allow an Assault Action from a greater distance, in which case, they do not need to be in 
base to base contact with a target model to make an attack. 
SIDEARMS
Sidearms may be used in Assault Actions, following the rules for resolving an Assault and using the Lethality value 
listed in the CQB profile.
RESOLVING AN ASSAULT
ASSAULT ACTIONS
No hit roll is needed in an Assault Action, as close proximity 
ensures a connection. However, the target can attempt to 
negate the assault by making a Situational Awareness test.
SITUATIONAL AWARENESS TEST
Each target of an Assault Action makes an Awareness Test. 
A successful test means the assault is ineffective against 
that model. Some weapons may apply modifiers to this 
test and might still have effects even if passed.
COVER AND AWARENESS TESTS
If at least half of the target element is within any cover 
during the assault, a +1 bonus is applied to their Situational 
Awareness tests.
ASSAULT FROM OUTSIDE FIELD OF FIRE AND PRONE
If a model is assaulted from outside their Field of Fire (FoF), 
or if they are prone, they automatically fail their Awareness 
Test.
LETHALITY TEST AND CASUALTIES
For each successful assault, the attacking player makes a 
Lethality test using the weapon’s Lethality statistic. Each 
successful Lethality test results in the removal of one 
model as a casualty. Casualties must be chosen from the 
models targeted by the Assault Action. If a specific weapon 
is used, the removed target model must be in contact with 
the attacking model that used it.

WEAPONS
DESIGNER’S NOTE
WEAPONS IN SPECTRE OPERATIONS
In the real world, weapons systems and their effectiveness are extremely varied. An M4 and an AK47 
will perform quite differently in terms of accuracy, range and penetration. In Spectre Operations, we 
have broken down and grouped together these systems into categories which are broadly defined 
by how the weapon is used on the battlefield. For these purposes, and M4 and an AK47 will be used 
in relatively similar situations, so they use the same profile. Other categories will give the profiles for 
more specialised weapons, which fit a particular role. We encourage the player, if they are familiar with 
real world firearms and other systems, to change a category if they feel it is better placed elsewhere. 
We have also assumed that most weapons are fitted with the correct optics and other accessories for 
their role, but these profiles can be modified to represent specialist ammunition, or other bespoke 
modifications using the specialist builds found in the Equipment section.

Weapons all have three ranges, with corresponding Ballistics Modifiers, Lethality, and Armour Penetration shown 
for each range, depicting their effectiveness and utility at different ranges. Each Weapon’s stat line will state 
these values. The CQB range of each weapon is 6” measured as up to and including the given value. Beyond this 
distance is considered to be in the Engagement range, up to and including the given value, after which is the 
weapon’s Extreme range. Extreme range is considered any distance over the Engagement range. If a value is given 
for the Extreme range of a weapon, this is the maximum distance an Engagement Action may be made with that 
weapon. Some weapons, such as sniper rifles, may have no value given in their Extreme range, in which case the 
Extreme range is unlimited.
Some weapons cannot be used in CQB or other ranges, in which case the value will be shown as (-).  
It is assumed that weapons designed for a particular role, such as Sniper Rifles, Short Assault Rifles etc., are set up 
with optics that optimise them in this capacity. 
AUTOMATIC
Automatic Weapons are usually Machine Guns or Sub 
Machine Guns. They often have a high rate of fire and 
can be belt or high capacity magazine fed. Often fired 
in short, controlled bursts, but when occasion calls, they 
can unleash a volume of fire onto a target. 
	
►
Whenever additional Momentum is spent 
on an Engagement Action, add two dice to 
the weapons Engagement, rather than 1. If a 
casualty is caused by an Engagement Action 
that includes an Automatic weapon, add an 
additional dice to the Target Elements Stress 
Test. 
SUPPRESSING
Suppressing Weapons lay down a large volume of fire 
into an area. 
	
►
Suppressing Weapons are used to pin an enemy 
in place, using precise or high volumes of fire. An 
Element that is required to make a Stress Test 
from that Engagement Action always counts as 
having an additional single failed test result for 
each Suppressing Weapon used.
SUSTAINED
Some Weapons are able to fire continuously at a target 
for a prolonged period of time. 
	
►
Weapons with this rule will be written as 
Sustained x where x is the number of dice 
you roll for accurate fire when making an 
Engagement action with this weapon. Each 
additional Momentum Spent will add an 
additional x to the Engagement. 
HEAVY 
Heavy Weapons are usually large and bulky, taking time 
to Manoeuvre. 
	
►
A Heavy Weapon may not be used in an 
Engagement Action if the model within the 
Element made a Manoeuvre Action in the same 
turn. A Model with a Heavy Weapon may not 
make an Engagement Action after Disembarking 
from a Vehicle. 
DEPLOYED
Deployed Weapons are usually mounted on a tripod, a 
vehicle or other Emplacement. 
	
►
Unless the Weapon is Mounted on a Vehicle, 
the Element with the Deployed Weapon must 
spend 1 Momentum to Pack Down the Weapon 
in order to make a Manoeuvre Action. While the 
Weapon is Packed Down, it must be carried by 
2 Designated Models within the Element and 
may not be used until 1 Momentum is spent to 
Deploy the Weapon for use.
COMPACT 
Compact Weapons are very easy to manoeuvre and fire, 
and perform well in confined areas. These weapons are 
often issued to vehicle crew, or fighters that are required 
to mount and dismount a vehicle quickly. 
	
►
Elements equipped with Compact Weapons may 
make Mavoeuvre Actions after disembarking a 
vehicle. 
	
►
Compact Weapons may be used to make an 
Engagement action in the same turn as a 
Embark/Disembark action.   
	
►
Compact Weapons do not have any negative 
modifiers for using additional Momentum on an 
Engagement Action, when used in CQB range.

►
Single Fire Weapons may only ever use 1 
Momentum per Engagement Action. 
ONE SHOT
One Shot Weapons are typically disposable shoulder fired 
launchers and can only be used once per game.   
SILENT

AOE WEAPONS AND VEHICLES
Weapons designed to engage vehicles often have the 
Area of Effect (AoE) rule. 
	
►
When targeting a vehicle, make an Engagement 
Action as normal. Do not use the AoE rules 
described above. Instead, if an AoE weapon scores 
a Glancing or Penetrating hit on a vehicle, add 
the AoE value to the dice result when rolling on 
the appropriate Glancing or Penetrating hit table.
AOE WEAPONS, AUTOMATIC & 
SUSTAINED.
If a Weapon has the AoE rule and the Automatic and 
Sustained Rule, each test for Accurate Fire will use the 
AoE rule. 
	
►
For Example, if a Weapon with AoE 3 and the 
Automatic Rule spends 2 Momentum to roll 
3 dice for Accurate Fire, each of those 3 shots 
will cause a potential 3 hits on an Element. If 
any of these shots are not Accurate, each shot 
will reduce the number of the AoE as normal. 
This makes weapons such as Multiple Grenade 
Launchers extremely lethal.
THERMOBARIC
Thermobaric Weapons ignore armour bonuses. 
	
►
In addition, Thermobaric Weapons will instantly 
add 2 stress to the Target Element that they 
are used against. Armour has no effect against 
Thermobaric Weapons and Situational Awareness 
tests may not be made against them.
	
►
Thermobaric Weapons will also add 2 additional 
dice when resolving Penetration against a civilian 
vehicle (this is cumulative with other rules, such 
as Armour Piercing).
AIRBURST
Airburst weapons will explode above the target, usually 
calculated by a simple onboard computer, or pre-
programmed by the shooter. This has the effect of 
negating any cover benefits an opponent may have.
	
►
Weapons with the Airburst rule will negate any 
benefits from cover when making Situational 
Awareness tests during Engagement Actions. 
AOE WEAPONS
AOE WEAPON EXAMPLE:
A Veteran Element (Ballistics 5) chooses 
to make an Engagement against a Militia 
Element using a Grenade Launcher (AoE 3). 
The Veteran rolls for accurate fire, resulting 
in a 6. The Engagement was made at 
24”, which is in the Extreme Range of the 
weapon, which confers a -2 to Accurate 
Fire. The result after modifiers is 4, but a 
5 is needed for the shot to be accurate. 
The shot still hits the Target Element, but 
because the Engagement failed the roll 
needed for Accurate fire by 1, it reduces 
the AoE value by 1, to AoE 2. This results in 
2 models in the Target Element being hit.
AREA OF EFFECT WEAPONS 
Many weapons have an explosive warhead, particulate 
dispersal or other method for causing destruction over a wide 
area, such as an extreme rate of fire.
	
►
These Weapons will have the AoE(x) rule, where the x 
value is the number of models in the Target Element 
that are hit as a result of each single Accurate Shot 
from a weapon with this rule. 
	
►
If an Engagement Action with an AoE weapon misses, 
it may still be Lethal to some of the Element, or if 
the roll was particularly low, a nearby element. For 
every value on the dice roll that the shot misses by, 
the AoE of the weapon is reduced by 1. For example: 
if the Weapon used has a value of AoE 3, the Ballistics 
of the Shooter is 7, and the roll to hit was a 5, after 
modifiers, the Engagement would still count as 
Accurate fire, but with a reduced AoE 1 (3-2). If the 
result would cause the AoE to become 0 or a negative 
number, the AoE weapon will have no effect.
FRAG
Frag Weapons will instantly add 1 additional stress on top of any others to the Target Element in an Engagement they 
are used in. 
	
►
If a Situational Awareness Test is Passed against a Frag Weapon, the hit will not be ignored as usual, but will 
hit with a -1 to Lethality. 
	
►
If the Element is at 3” or more Cohesion, the Lethality will be reduced by 1. 
	
►
Frag Weapons will also add 1 additional dice when resolving Penetration against a civilian vehicle (this is 
cumulative with other rules, such as Armour Penetrating). When making Stress Tests after an Engagement, 
add 1 additional dice to the Stress Test.

This Section will detail Equipment a model or element can be equipped with and how they will benefit, or

BODY ARMOUR
Body armour usually consists of a plate carrier and helmet, or just a plate carrier as a minimum. Although there 
are a variety of different types of these, some far more advanced than others, for the purposes of these rules they 
all have broadly the same effect. 
Body armour will reduce the Lethality of a weapon by 1, to a minimum of 10+. 
COVERT BODY ARMOUR
This armour usually consists of a very slim, low profile 
plate carrier that can be worn under clothing. Unless you 
know what to look for, this armour is usually discreet 
enough to go unnoticed. This armour will not add any 
modifier to the roll when testing to maintain Cover.
HEAVY BODY ARMOUR
Some Body Armour is scaled up to gain additional 
protection against smaller calibre and fragmentation 
impacts. These can be the additional soft plates, visored 
helmets and layered fragmentation protection suits. 
These are usually used in Anti-Terror operations where 
these threats are more likely to be encountered. 
In addition to the benefits of Body Armour, this armour 
will further reduce the Lethality of any weapons with 
the Frag rule by 1, to a minimum of 10, in addition to 
any other modifiers, such as Situational Awareness Tests 
against Frag Weapons. 
EOD SUIT
An EOD Suit is extremely heavy and cumbersome, but 
is designed to protect its wearer from close proximity 
Fragmentation Blasts. Although not worn in direct 
combat, these suits are usually used to defuse ordnance 
or IEDs.
The wearer of an EOD Suit will reduce the Lethality of a 
Fragmentation Weapon by -3. The Wearer also has their 
Movement Value reduced by -2.
BALLISTIC SHIELDS
Shields are used in modern combat on certain 
occasions. Although more frequently seen in use by 
Law Enforcement Agencies, nations such as Russia use 
them for particular purposes in military engagements, 
domestic or foreign. These can broadly be categorised 
as Full Shields, covering almost all of the bearer, or Half 
Shields, which cover the torso and head, or less.
FULL SHIELD
A model with a Full Shield will have a greater degree of 
protection when facing small arms fire from the front. 
A model with a Full Shield will reduce the lethality of 
any hits by -1 where they are the Target Element in an 
Engagement action, if the attack originates from the FoF 
of the Target Element.
This is cumulative with other items such as body 
armour. If an Element has at least 1 model for every 
3 with a Full Shield, that Element will benefit from an 
additional +2 to their Situational Awareness Tests.
HALF SHIELD 
A model with a Half Shield will have a greater degree of 
protection when facing small arms fire from the front. 
A model with a Half Shield will reduce the lethality of 
the first time the Element hits by -1 in which they can 
be the target from any Engagement action which this 
model is a target of and originates from the FoF of the 
model with the shield.

REMOTE WEAPONS SYSTEMS.
Remote Weapons Systems (RWS) are an increasingly 
common feature of armoured and even civilian 
vehicles. These systems can broadly be classified into 
3 categories, Light, Medium and Heavy. Whatever the 
system, the following rules will apply when making an 
Engagement with the weapon.
RWS will have no modifier to hit when additional 
momentum is applied to an Engagement using the 
Weapon. The Weapon will also gain the benefits for 
MK3 Hybrid Thermal NVGs (page 58) to represent 
their advanced Optics and Targeting Systems. An 
Engagement Action will also suffer no penalty from a 
Manoeuvre Action being made before the Engagement, 
due to the stabilisation of the weapon and ballistics 
assisting computer.
LIGHT RWS.
These systems can be mounted onto Small Unmanned 
Ground Vehicles and all other Vehicles . A single Light or 
Medium Machine Gun can be mounted onto the System. 
MEDIUM RWS
This System broadly encompasses the majority of 
Remote Weapons Systems available. These may be 
mounted on Large Unmanned Ground Vehicles and all 
other vehicle types. A Medium RWS can mount a single 
Machine Gun (Light, Medium or Heavy), Automatic 
Grenade Launcher, Minigun, Anti-Tank Launcher, or 
30mm Cannon. The RWS can also be equipped with 
Smoke Dispensers.
HEAVY RWS
These Systems are Specialist in nature and can have 
multiple weapons mounted on them to engage a large 
variety of threats.
A Heavy RWS can include a Single 20mm, 30mm or 
40mm Cannon or Chain Gun, 1-2 Guided Anti-Tank 
Launchers and a Light or Medium Machine Gun.
STABILISED GUN
An Engagement Action will also suffer no penalty from a 
Manoeuvre Action being made before the Engagement, 
due to the stabilisation of the weapon and ballistics 
assisting computer.
PINTLE MOUNTED WEAPON
Many Vehicles have a turret or pintle mounted weapon 
which can be fired by a gunner, at the risk of exposing 
themselves to enemy fire and the risk of having an open 
hatch on a vehicle.
If a Pintle Mounted Weapon is used in an Engagement, 
the AV of the vehicle will be reduced by -1 for the rest 
of the turn.
EXPLOSIVE REACTIVE ARMOUR
Explosive Reactive Armour (ERA) was developed to 
counter ever more advanced Anti-Armour weapons. It 
works, in simple terms, by detonating a brick of armour 
on the hull of a vehicle when it is struck by a large 
calibre or explosive weapon. There are various versions 
of this armour, some very advanced and able to defeat 
Explosive and Kinetic Weapons. The downside to this 
armour is that it is extremely dangerous for infantry to 
be in proximity to the vehicle when it is activated.
ERA will increase the Vehicle Class by 1 against all 
Weapons with the Anti Armour or Anti Tank Rules.
If a weapon with the Anti Armour or Anti Tank Rule is 
used in an Engagement with a Vehicle with ERA, infantry 
Elements within 3” of the Vehicle will suffer an AoE 3 hit, 
with a Lethality of 6. 
NEXT GENERATION ERA
This Reactive Armour, in addition to the rules listed 
above will also increase the Vehicle Class by 1 against 
Kinetic Penetrator Weapons. Weapons with the Tandem 
and Kinetic Penetrator rule will gain no benefit from 
their special rules against a vehicle with Next Generation 
ERA.

TASK FORCE BUILDER
DESIGNER’S NOTE: TASK FORCE
The Task Force Builder is intended to represent a coalition of ‘Western’ style militaries. You could use this 
list to build a Task Force of British Army soldiers (Enlisted), supporting a UK Ranger Regiment (Tier 2) or 
SAS (Tier 1) strike force. 
It is important to be aware that although the capability of Tier 2 and Tier 1 Elements are extremely 
high, the access to some equipment is more limited than Veteran or Enlisted Elements. This is intended 
to represent the roles these highly specialised units take in real life, rather than their actual access to 
equipment and encourage the same use in Spectre Operations. You’ll find using Enlisted, Veteran or Tier 2 
Elements to provide fire support from heavy weapons and vehicles, while Tier 1 Elements equipped with 
rifles attack an objective, a very flexible and ‘realistic’ way of playing.
This list can also be used to build NATO adjacent forces, such as Russian and Chinese armies, although you 
may wish to modify them based on personal knowledge and/or experience.
Task Force units represent the cutting edge of modern military capability: highly trained, heavily resourced, 
and supported by an array of advanced technologies. These elite formations are usually composed of Tier 
1 and Tier 2 Operators, veterans of special operations who conduct missions where precision, speed, and 
intelligence are paramount. Whether acting under military command, intelligence agencies, or private 
contractors, Task Force teams operate with maximum efficiency and minimal footprint.
Task Force Elements are designed to conduct rapid intervention, counter-terror operations, covert surveillance, 
hostage rescues, or high-value target elimination. With access to advanced equipment, specialist training, and 
integrated communications, they are capable of dominating the battlespace through superior coordination 
and force multiplication. Every shot counts. Every movement is planned. Every escalation is deliberate.
Operating a Task Force on the tabletop means planning for escalation, maximizing asymmetric advantage, and 
managing risk with surgical intent. You won’t have numbers but you will have control, speed, and lethality.

PRECISION AND OVERMATCH
“One call. One impact. And the battlefield changes shape.”
Task Force elements represent the most elite and well-equipped forces on the modern battlefield, with near-
unparalleled access to global support networks, high-altitude surveillance, and surgical strike capabilities. 
Escalations for a Task Force aren’t just backup—they are a deliberate application of overwhelming force, timed 
with intelligence, coordination, and technological superiority.
Whether it’s a single loitering munition removing a key target, a gunship dominating an urban skyline, or 
satellite-guided rockets flattening an enemy position, Task Force Escalations provide high-impact tactical 
options with minimal footprint and maximum control.
However, every escalation changes the nature of the fight. Calling in drones, guided artillery, or close air support 
can compromise stealth, alter enemy alert states, or risk collateral damage. Used wisely, they turn a losing 
position into total dominance. Used recklessly, they escalate the conflict beyond recovery.
Escalations represent off-map assets delivered through JTACs, Commanders, or specialist-equipped Elements. 
Coordination, line of sight, and correct equipment—such as Long-Range Comms or Laser Designators—are crucial 
to success.

DESIGNER’S NOTE: PLAY IT YOUR WAY
We’ve designed these scenarios to allow you to complete the objective in the way you like to play. You can 
go in all guns blazing and complete the objective through sheer force, or play a stealth mission, where a 
small team of Covert Operators carefully and quietly advance on the objective and neutralise it. 
We strongly encourage players to experiment with these scenarios and the approaches to them - no two 
games will be the same.
Also, experiment with the forces involved and the resources limit. Two Task Force players can easily face 
off against each other, with one designated the attacker and one the defender. Likewise, multiple players 
could play each scenario to compete for a common objective.
LOOKING FOR MORE NARRATIVE?
Spectre Operations encourages narrative and 
story driven scenarios and campaigns. 
Scenarios can be linked into a series of narrative 
events. For instance, a Secure Intel Scenario can 
be played, and depending on the amount of 
intel gathered, the attacker may choose to next 
play a Compound Assault or Ambush Scenario.

could even chose to have the Defenders start 
the game Alert - to represent them expecting 
an attack. 
In this chapter we also provide an example of

An Enemy Commander has been located and must be killed or captured. 
They have a Force at their command, which can be engaged and 
neutralised if required. 
AREA OF OPERATIONS
TABLE SIZE
2x2 or Greater.
TERRAIN.

ATTACKER

may perform an SSE Action while within the compound to collect Intel. 
For each Point of Momentum spent, 1 piece of Intel is gathered. Each 
piece of Intel gathered generates 20r.
DEFENDER
Primary Objective: If the Commander is alive and uncaptured at the 
end of the mission, the Defender gains 100r.
Secondary Objective: Escape the area. If the Commander is moved off 
of a table edge, while embarked in a vehicle, the game ends and the 
Defender wins and gains an additional 100r

The Game will end at the end of Turn 6.

Resources Wins.

OPERATION VAGABOND
A cargo ship heading towards the Panama Canal is suspected of being hijacked by non-state actors. Intelligence 
recovered in an operation conducted in Yemen suggests that the intention of the hijackers is to detonate a device 
that was previously loaded onto the ship in a shipping container. 
The exact nature of the device is unknown, but it is suspected the device is to be detonated while in the canal, 
causing catastrophic disruption to shipping and global trade.
The hijackers are assumed to be keeping the crew alive in order not to arouse suspicion via comms and maintaining 
procedure. It is suspected that a high profile commander is leading the Hijackers. It is desirable that he is captured 
and brought in for interrogation.
The ship is currently in a queue to entre the canal. It is a race against the clock to stop the attack.
Task Force Plymouth has been dispatched to approach the ship using covert submersibles and board the cargo ship 
under cover of darkness.

VISUALISING THE CAMPAIGN
The main location of the Campaign can be decided 
mutually between players, or based on the scenery 
available to you. The Insurgency can take play in any 
environment from the Mountains of Central Asia, the 
cities of the Middle East, Africa or South America. Deep in 
the Rain Forests of the Yucatan or South East Asia, or the 
dense urban cities of Europe, America or Asia. 
Some Scenarios may dictate certain locations (such as a 
cargo ship, or penthouse of a skyscraper). Feel free to edit 
the locations based on your collection or the settings you 
have chosen. The important aspects are the Objectives in 
each Scenario.
STARTING A CAMPAIGN
To start the campaign, each player will build their Total 
Forces for the campaign. 
This Total Force represents all of the assets available to 
the commander.
Each Scenario will have a Resources Limit, which will 
dictate how much of your Total Force you are able to 
allocate to that particular mission. 
It is important to choose which Elements you use for each 
scenario wisely, if they are lost during the game, they 
may not be easy to replace.
The Task Force may spend up to 5000r on their 
Task Force, with Elements chosen from the Task 
Force Builder. 
The Insurgency Player may spend up to 10000r 
on their Insurgency, with Elements from the Militia 
Force Builder.
CAMPAIGNS
PLAYING A CAMPAIGN
A Campaign is a series of scenarios played by two or more players. The outcome of each game will have the potential 
to degrade or expand each Force. After each game, Resources will be gained, which can be used to buy or recruit new 
Elements after a Scenario has been played.
The length of the Campaign can be dictated by time in the real world, or by agreeing an amount of Scenarios to be 
played. 
For example, players may decide to play as many games as they can over a 3 month period, or to simply play 6 games in 
the campaign.
Experienced players may wish to create a fully thematic Campaign, with an overarching storyline or multiple interwoven 
plots, or even base a Campaign on a historic or real world one.
Books, Movies and Videogames are great inspiration for playing this way.
This Section of Spectre Operations will give you guidelines on how to run a campaign between two or more forces. If you 
are new to Spectre Operations and Campaign play, we suggest one player takes control of a Task Force and another player 
assumes the role of an Insurgent, Untrained Force.
Campaigns can easily be adapted to take place between Untrained Forces, such as two rival Warlords or with multiple 
Trained Forces, such as a NATO Coalition force against a Neer Peer Russian or Chinese Force.

CAMPAIGN LOSSES
Elements will start to suffer if they gain casualties in a Scenario. 
When playing a Scenario, record any models that are Killed in a game from Each Element. These fighters are permanently 
removed from the Element and they may only be replaced when Recruiting after a Scenario has been played. 
	
►
In addition, for each model that was Killed in the Element, that Element’s starting Stress is permanently 
increased by 1.  
	
►
If a fighter is Wounded at the end of the game, the following effects apply:
Incapacitated: The Fighter may not rejoin the Element, but the Stress of the Element is not increased.
Minor Injury: The Fighter may only rejoin the Element at the end of the Chapter.
Narrow Escape: The Fighter must spend the next Scenario recovering, after which he may rejoin the Element.
	
►
1xp If the Element was not Routed, Destroyed or Captured in a Scenario.
	
►
1xp if the Element removed a Stress Effect in a Scenario

2xp if the Element Routed, Destroyed or Captured an Element in a Scenario

EXPERIENCE.
Forces that fight in Operations will gain Experience Points (xp) over the course of the Campaign. Each Element will gain 
the following amount of Experience after each Scenario:
UNTRAINED:
Hardened: The Element may Re-Roll any amount of dice 
once when making a Stress Test.
Guerillas: The Element may Re-Roll any amount of dice 
once when making a Situational Awareness Test.
Tank Hunters: The Element may Re-Roll any amount 
of dice once when determining Accurate fire in an 
Engagement when targeting a vehicle Element.
TRAINED:
Counter Insurgency: The Element may Re-Roll any 
amount of dice once when making an Ballistics test in an 
Engagement when targeting an Infantry Element. 
Overwatch Team: The Element may Re-Roll any amount 
of dice once when determining Accurate fire when making 
a Snap Fire Reaction.
Urban Assault team: The Element may Re-Roll any 
amount of dice once when determining Accurate fire 
when making an Engagement in CQB.
VETERAN ELEMENTS FOR HIRE 
You may become attached to certain Elements during a campaign, they may have performed heroic feats or had 
particularly bad luck! Either way, you may wish to keep Elements and all of their gained Experience after a Campaign to 
use in a future Campaign. 
If you wish to keep an Element after a Campaign, increase the Resources cost for the Element by 10r for Each point of 
Experience they have spent on Statistics increases or Skills.
