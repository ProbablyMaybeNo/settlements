## Route questions to the appropriate method

| Question | Best first method | Then |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does the formula match the rule? | Deterministic/exact conformance | A player example verifies the explanation. |
| Does a combo dominate many legal contexts? | Targeted simulation / counterbuilds | Humans test counterplay and decisions. |
| Do players notice and choose a reaction? | Observed human session | Improve explanation or investigate why it is avoided. |
| Does the game feel fast, bloody and tactical? | Full human battles, timings and concrete moments | Correlate with functional turns and attrition. |
| Can the packet teach without help? | Fresh unguided groups | Revise and repeat with new readers. |
| Do real templates and models work? | Physical print/terrain test | Match the remote implementation to the physical rules. |

Old engine results, large run counts and passing software tests do not validate today's game by themselves. Simulation can have policy and coverage blind spots. Human feedback can have skill, social and sampling biases. Record both rather than allowing one to automatically overrule the other.

## Shared identifiers

Use rule owner IDs, packet/rules hashes, crew/board/scenario revisions, an issue ID and the tested variant. A human report of an oppressive interaction can create a simulation hypothesis; a simulated extreme can create a human vignette. Neither silently edits the Master.
