# Host protocol

## Summary

A practical before/during/after session procedure with light and instrumented logging.

^testing-summary

## Before

- [ ] Select guided, focused, integration or unguided mode. Do not call a taught game blind.
- [ ] Write one or two test questions, relevant owner IDs and expected evidence.
- [ ] Freeze rules, PDF hash, roster, scenario and board revision; assign session and pair IDs.
- [ ] Check board dimensions, deployment, terrain properties, objectives, templates and unit IDs.
- [ ] Recruit appropriate players and log prior experience/repeat play, without collecting unnecessary personal details.
- [ ] Explain that the game is being tested, not the participant. Ask permission before recording audio/video; participation and recording are separate choices.
- [ ] Give the small participant pack and a stop time. Keep host hypotheses and answers off the table.

## Suggested first-session timebox

Book about two hours provisionally: 20 minutes setup/arrival, 10 initial teaching, 75 play, 10 feedback and 5 tidy-up. This is scheduling headroom, **not a measured Settlements playtime promise**. Record setup, teaching and play separately. An unfinished game is useful evidence; mark it incomplete rather than assigning an invented result.

## During

Observe choices, hesitations and rule lookups. Use neutral prompts such as: What are you trying to do? What did you expect to happen? What options do you see? Avoid asking whether a particular change would fix the design while the game is still underway.

In a guided session, teach the initial core loop and answer necessary questions; record interventions. In an unguided session, let the players try the packet first. If they cannot proceed, log the obstruction and the rescue; the rest of the battle can continue as **assisted**, without pretending the unguided task passed.

Do not argue with a reaction, correct an opinion or explain why a rule is actually clever. A player solution is a hypothesis; the event and its effect are evidence. Note positive moments too, so improvements preserve what works.

## Two logging tiers

| Tier | Who records | Minimum data |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light - default | Host | Versions, participants by ID, board/crew/side, timings, per-round VP, outcome/completion, major confusion, intervention and 5-question individual feedback. |
| Instrumented - focused session | Observer | Everything above plus selected mechanic opportunities, uses, results, actions/resources and a small declared sample of attacks/activations. |

Do not make players record every die while they are learning. If no observer is available, use the light tier or record a consenting session for later sampling. Never fabricate missing tallies from memory.

## After

1. Players complete the short exit sheet **individually before group discussion**.
2. Debrief concrete moments, including why an option was not used. Separate misunderstood text from unwanted behaviour.
3. Record winner, draw or incomplete state, first player, scenario role, rule deviations and the key question's result.
4. Transcribe raw measurements without rewriting criticism. Store screenshots/recordings by session ID only where permitted.
5. Create or merge issue records, keep contradictory evidence and schedule a retest.
6. Thank testers and offer a brief change summary later. If recruiting through a reciprocal group, test others' games too.

## Stop or rescope

Stop when requested, when the group cannot continue or when the remaining time cannot answer the question. A blocked or abandoned test is not a failed participant. A lethal opening or a long stalemate is a finding, not a reason to hide the session.

Method background: [Choose the right playtest method](https://gamesuserresearch.com/choose-the-right-playtest-method/), [Find usability issues in games with playtests](https://gamesuserresearch.com/find-usability-issues-in-games-with-playtests/). The procedure and time allocations here are tailored recommendations.
