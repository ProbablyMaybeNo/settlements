# Testing programme

## Summary

A staged first learning cycle, followed by broader balance, pricing and release checks.

^testing-summary

## Design questions first

Settlements aims for grounded, fast-paced, bloody tactical battles with meaningful options and concise rules. Test whether the table produces that experience; do not treat high lethality or long duration as inherently correct. Operational targets below are **proposed testing criteria**, not adopted rules or advertised playtime.

## First cycle - a learning budget, not a release quota

| Wave | Initial budget | Main question | Move on when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 - preflight | 2 solo dry runs | Can the packet, board and counters resolve a battle? | No missing essential profile, scoring rule or unresolved action. |
| 1 - guided baseline | 4 full games, at least 2 player pairs | Can players choose legal actions and resolve the core loop after the initial explanation? | The largest recurring comprehension and pacing problems are identified and addressed. |
| 2 - isolate and integrate | 6 short focused sessions plus 4 full games | What causes a specific problem, and does its remedy survive normal play? | Critical interactions have both intended-use and integration evidence. |
| 3 - first unguided wave | 3 fresh groups, 1 full battle each; begin with one group | Can the packet teach setup, play and victory without designer rescue? | Latest packet supports independent setup and a resolved outcome; investigate each rescue/contradiction. |
| 4 - regression | 4-8 full games | Did approved fixes improve play without breaking adjacent systems? | No unresolved major blocker; improvements recur with fresh and returning players. |

This proposes **15-19 external full games**, plus 6 short sessions and 2 solo dry runs. Those are distinct session types, not all complete games. They may expose major redesign needs; extend or repeat a wave where evidence demands it. Do not count games on different rules versions as one balance sample.

## Then expand

1. Rotate movement, stealth, grenades, cover, armour and melee/ALERT modules while keeping most of each roster stable.
2. Add hacking/interactive terrain, then deployables and selected skill combinations in small fixed packs.
3. Test different headcounts, asymmetric roles and advanced builds. Catalogue items absent from early packs are **unmeasured**, not approved by association.
4. Use the simulation programme to identify extreme probabilities and combinations. Use human play for comprehension, handling and experience.
5. Rebuild prices only after the mechanical baseline is reasonably stable. Then repeat real legal **equal-Credit** roster tests; mechanical testing before prices cannot establish purchase balance.
6. Campaign rewards, recruitment growth, injuries, recovery and runaway economies need a separate linked-battle campaign phase after their systems and costs are ready.

## Per-session research brief

Choose **one primary and at most one secondary question**. Write expected behaviour, a way to encounter it, what to record and what would challenge the expectation. Example: does reserving ALERT create useful counterplay without making players afraid to activate? Measure reaction opportunities/uses and lost actions, then ask about choices. Do not ask the player to endorse a preferred answer.

## Release-readiness gates

- [ ] Latest rules snapshot and public test packet agree; every used profile is present.
- [ ] Essential mechanics have observed intended use, failure, counterplay and full-battle integration.
- [ ] Every rule/profile intended for release has an explicit coverage disposition; no silent unsupported content.
- [ ] Several independent fresh groups can learn the latest packet without rescue. Three is an initial checkpoint, not statistical proof.
- [ ] No open critical or major unresolved issue; severe reproducible exploits have a recorded decision and retest.
- [ ] Repeat play, objective variety, terrain variety and fresh-player comprehension have evidence, not only designer games.
- [ ] Point costs have received their own equal-Credit testing after rebuild.
- [ ] Physical play has verified measuring, facing, templates, tokens and terrain, including vertical play.
- [ ] Final edited wording and aids have passed an unguided regression, not just an earlier draft.
- [ ] Remaining limitations are documented and judged acceptable for the intended release scope.

## Review cadence

Review at the end of each small wave. Freeze a version during comparable games; do not edit rules halfway through a measurement batch. A safety/resolve-the-game ruling may be made and logged, but tag the game as affected. Reproduce major concerns, choose the smallest effective approved change, then retest.
