# Boards crews and fixtures

## Summary

Control the environment without turning every session into an artificial duel.

^testing-summary

## Baseline

Begin with Hold the Block and its mirrored six-fighter crews in the starter PDF. Keep board geometry, deployment, objective placement, equipment, skill allocation and scoring fixed for the first comparisons. Log any approximation. These rosters are equally equipped, not validated 750-Credit builds.

Swap **players, deployment sides and who acts first** deliberately across a pair; record what changed. A genuine initiative advantage must be reported, not simply averaged away. Follow ordinary initiative in ordinary games; controlled first-player assignment belongs only to a clearly labelled diagnostic fixture, not an unannounced rules change.

## Board family rotation

| Family | What it reveals |
| --- | --- |
| Dense mixed urban | Core reference: covered approaches, meaningful sight lines and flanks. |
| Industrial / vertical | Roof access, height, falls, lifts, crane/bridge positioning and real model handling. |
| Interiors / compound | Doors, facing, smoke/LOS, close combat and short-range tools. |
| Courtyard / longer lanes | Crossing risk, range value, cover scarcity and counterplay. |
| Interactive machinery | Terminals, manual controls and changing access/LOS. |
| Chokepoints with alternative paths | Mines, turrets, displacement, stealth and whether denial can be answered. |

Record board size, objective/scenario revision, terrain height, longest useful firing lane, route lengths, accessible roofs, choke width and available alternative approaches. Terrain count alone does not describe density. Open or near-impenetrable extremes are sensitivity tests, not the only environment to average.

## Three ready-to-run focused fixtures

### F01 - Cover comparison (M01)

Use the same basic rifleman and target profile, first in open view, then behind Light and Heavy cover. Keep range, facing and Stress fixed. Confirm players calculate each test correctly; count only the shots declared in the measured block. Then play an objective battle where they choose their own positions. Fixed die examples teach/check resolution; random trials measure outcomes. Do not mix them in the same rate.

### F02 - Reaction choice (M03)

Place an ALERT defender with a visible attacker in range and an alternative approach route. Run an incoming shot and a Charge case, then switch roles. Record whether Return Fire/Dive/normal timing is identified, what it costs and whether the player felt there was a useful choice. Do not force the preferred response and then report spontaneous use.

### F03 - Recovery under pressure (M04)

Use a multi-Wound leader, a single-Wound fighter, a medkit carrier, nearby cover and an enemy with a firing option. Exercise a non-wounding hit, lost Wound, Down, revival and the two-round Down timer using declared rule-check dice if needed. For a subsequent random or full-game measurement, reset the fixture and label it separately. Record when a unit can contribute again and what the rescue cost.

## Advanced fixture discipline

Make one supplemental sheet containing all needed procedures and exact approved profiles. Enforce rank/Stat, skill unlock, hands/slots, operator and action constraints. Use analytical illegal extremes only when explicitly labelled; they cannot establish legal roster dominance. Do not invent missing machinery or scenario rules as if approved.

Physical vertical play is mandatory eventually; an actual miniatures developer reported relevant terrain concerns emerging after remote alpha testing: [Firefight Playtest Designer Notes: Part One](https://www.manticgames.com/news/firefight-playtest-designer-notes-part-one/).
