# 🧭 Candidate Games — a sourcing shortlist

> **What this is.** A discovery-run sweep for skirmish games we don't have yet, cast against the same profile as [[Spectre Operations]] and [[Zona Alfa]]: small crews (5–15 models, individually activated), modern-through-near-future grounded mil-tech, dense terrain, objective-driven missions, campaign-capable. Publisher catalogues, BGG, forums, itch.io/Wargame Vault, and designer blogs — breadth over depth.
>
> **What this is not.** Not a capture run. **No hub rows, no per-game notes.** Nothing here has been read — every line is "worth reading," not "read." When one of these gets captured, [[Wargaming Research Hub]] and `research/sources/` are where the real work lands.

> [!warning] Anti-hallucination discipline
> Every game below is confirmed to exist by a real, citable source — its URL is given. Anything about *how a game plays* that I didn't verify directly is tagged **[CONSENSUS]** (repeated across reviews) or **[INFERENCE]** (my own read of the blurbs), never stated as fact. Two games below were caught by the same trap that snagged Judge Dredd last time — see **Probably not**.

---
## 🎯 Closest to Settlements

1. **[[#⭐ Scrappers|Scrappers]]** (Osprey Games) — the single closest structural match in this sweep. A post-apocalyptic **Scrapper Crew** scavenges a table salvaging Scrap while three **Lifeform Types** (True Human/Mutant/Synthetic) gate traits and gear, D10-based, commander-driven activation, campaign-native. This is Settlements' scavenge-and-crew loop, published, by the same house as [[Zona Alfa]] and [[Mad Dogs with Guns]].
2. **[[#⭐ Skirmish Sangin|Skirmish Sangin]]** — the closest *tonal and scale* match: modern grounded combined-arms, 8–16 miniatures a side, fireteam radio comms, full ORBATs for both a professional force and an insurgency, off-table fire support. No sci-fi at all — this is what "2051 minus the tech tier" looks like on a table today.
3. **[[#⭐ Cyberpunk RED — Combat Zone|Cyberpunk RED: Combat Zone]]** — the most mechanically novel: **no rounds, no turns** — a continuous action economy where a model's pool of colour-coded Actions (green→yellow→red) shrinks as it takes wounds, so "how hurt are you" and "how much can you still do this activation" are the same track. Officially licensed, actively sold.

Honourable mention to two games already on our want-list that this run re-confirmed as genuinely worth the sourcing effort: **[[#Confirm-and-enrich — already on the want list|Reality's Edge]]** (cyberpunk hacking-crew skirmish, same author as [[#This Is Not a Test|This Is Not a Test]]) and **[[#Confirm-and-enrich — already on the want list|Black Ops]]** (espionage skirmish with a hacker character class and a card-driven activation deck).

## ⭐ Strong match

### ⭐ Scrappers
**Scrappers: Post-Apocalyptic Skirmish Wargames** · Osprey Games · Robert A. Faust, Andrew N. Davies, David Auden Nash · 2017.
*Scrapper Crews scavenge Ancient technology across a wasteland 150 years post-apocalypse, contested by True Humans, Mutants, and self-aware Synthetics.*
**Why it matches:** small crew, campaign-native, scavenging-as-the-objective, D10 engine — the shape is closer to Settlements than anything else found this run.
**The mechanic:** `[CONSENSUS — multiple independent reviews agree, not a direct rulebook read]` **Lifeform Type** (Human/Mutant/Synthetic) is the single gate that gates Trait and Gear access — one tag answering the job three separate lists would otherwise do. Missions place 6–15 Scrap tokens on a standard 4'×4' table as the shared objective.
**Availability:** in print — Osprey Games catalogue + PDF (DriveThruRPG, Osprey's own store).
**Source:** [Osprey product page](https://www.ospreypublishing.com/us/scrappers-9781472819277/) · [BGG entry](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/226048/scrappers-post-apocalyptic-skirmish-wargames)

### ⭐ Skirmish Sangin
Radio Dishdash Publishing · designer `[NOT FOUND]` · year `[INFERENCE — ~2012/13, dated from a Jan-2013 review post]`.
*Modern Afghanistan skirmish rules: NATO fireteams vs. insurgent militias, ~8–16 models a side, a ~2-hour game.*
**Why it matches:** this is Settlements' *tone and scale* with the sci-fi stripped out — grounded, narrative-first, fireteam-level, built around comms and off-table support rather than a big model count.
**The mechanic:** `[CONSENSUS]` full ORBATs for USA/UK/French/NZ/Australian regulars *and* the insurgent side, with rules for inter-fireteam radio communication and off-table assets (snipers, artillery, air) — the "supporting cast doesn't need a model" problem solved for a grounded setting.
**Availability:** PDF + 172pp softcover via Wargame Vault / DriveThruRPG.
**Source:** [Wargame Vault listing](https://www.wargamevault.com/product/109666/Skirmish-Sangin) · [Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.com/Skirmish-Sangin-Modern-Afghanistan-Wargaming/dp/0473281392)

### ⭐ Cyberpunk RED — Combat Zone
Monster Fight Club · designer Aaron Dill · officially licensed with R. Talsorian Games · 2022 (expansion *Cyberpunk Edgerunners: Combat Zone* shipping ~Jan 2025).
*Edgerunner gangs and mercs fight in Night City's Combat Zones, 2045.*
**Why it matches:** near-future, grounded-but-augmented mil-tech (cyberware, drones, corp security), gang-vs-gang skirmish at crew scale.
**The mechanic:** `[FACT — publisher's own product description]` *"act and react in organic, free-flowing actions with no rounds, no turns — the game just goes."* Each model's Actions are colour-coded green/yellow/red, rolling progressively worse dice or moving shorter distances as the model gets hurt — wound state and action economy are the same resource, which is a genuinely different answer to a problem Settlements solves with Stress instead.
**Availability:** in print, sold directly by Monster Fight Club.
**Source:** [Monster Fight Club product page](https://monsterfightclub.com/products/combat-zone) · [Kickstarter](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/monsterfightclub/cyberpunk-red-combat-zone)

### ⭐ Force on Force / Tomorrow's War
Ambush Alley Games, published in partnership with Osprey · Shawn Carpenter (+ Robby Carpenter) · Force on Force 2007 (self-published) / Osprey 2nd ed. ~2011; Tomorrow's War (the near-future sci-fi variant on the same engine) 2011.
*Force on Force resolves asymmetric modern-warfare skirmishes (COIN, urban insurgency); Tomorrow's War reuses the same engine for near-future combined-arms sci-fi.*
**Why it matches:** explicitly built for asymmetric warfare between a professional force and an irregular one — Settlements' entire premise — with a scalable engine that already covers "modern" and "near-future" as two settings on one chassis.
**The mechanic:** `[CONSENSUS]` a **Confidence/Force Morale** system that governs how much of the force is even willing to fight this turn, separate from any individual model's own nerve — a squad-level layer above individual Stress.
**Availability:** Osprey/Ambush Alley partnership has ended; **PDF still sold via Wargame Vault**, physical copies now secondary-market only (eBay/Noble Knight/AbeBooks).
**Source:** [Wargame Vault — Tomorrow's War](https://www.wargamevault.com/en/product/373328/tomorrow-s-war) · [Blood and Spectacles review](http://bloodandspectacles.blogspot.com/2017/03/review-tomorrows-warforce-on-force.html)

### ⭐ Across the Dead Earth
Dead Earth Games · Rich Chappell · year `[INFERENCE — ~2014, dated from a Dec-2014 review post]`.
*Three generations after "The Last War," Gangs of survivors fight over loot, territory and glory in a UK-set wasteland.*
**Why it matches:** explicitly the *grittier, less exaggerated* post-apoc lane — realism, scarcity, and "every bullet counts" is a direct quote from a roundup review, which is Settlements' own lethality target.
**The mechanic:** `[CONSENSUS]` **Event Cards** that actively disrupt the opponent's turn rather than just adding random flavour — a card layer sitting on top of an otherwise conventional activation system.
**Availability:** PDF/print via DriveThruRPG, Wargame Vault, and Hayland Terrain (bundled with minis).
**Source:** [DriveThruRPG listing](https://www.drivethrurpg.com/en/product/174475/across-the-dead-earth) · [Miniature Wargaming review](https://miniaturewargaming.com/blog/across-the-dead-earth-post-apocalyptic-skirmish-rules/)

## ⚙️ Worth a look

### Combat Zone
eM4 Miniatures · designer `[NOT FOUND]` · year `[NOT FOUND]`.
*A "dark near future" where megacorporations wall off cities; SWAT teams, gangs, mercenaries and machines fight over the ruins in 28mm.*
**Why it matches:** near-future, corporate-controlled-city setting is a close cousin of Settlements' contested-neighbourhood premise; a distinct "machines" faction reaches toward the robots axis.
**The mechanic:** `[INFERENCE — from the publisher's own marketing copy, not a rules read]` an **action-point** economy — no further mechanical detail was recoverable without the rulebook itself.
**Availability:** in print, boxed starter (~$43) + expandable faction/mini line, sold direct by eM4.
**Source:** [eM4 Miniatures — Combat Zone](https://em4miniatures.com/en-us/collections/combat-zone-the-game)

### Fireteam: Modern
Self-published via Wargame Vault · Rory Crabb · current "2020 edition."
*Modern combined-arms land combat, 15–28mm, fireteam-level activation, forces from roughly platoon size with light vehicle/artillery support, playable across any post-WW2-to-near-future conflict.*
**Why it matches:** fireteam-scale activation and a genre range that explicitly includes "the near future."
**The mechanic:** `[CONSENSUS — multiple blog reviews of the 2020 revision agree]` the 2020 edition replaced **activation dice with activation pips**, removing the failure mode where a low-quality unit simply never gets to act — a different fix for the same "dead activation" problem any dice-based order economy has to solve.
**Availability:** PDF via Wargame Vault.
**Source:** [Wargame Vault listing](https://www.wargamevault.com/product/196344/FireteamModern--Modern-Miniature-Wargame-Rules) · [Dougie's Wargaming Blog review of the 2020 edition](https://dougieswargamingblog.blogspot.com/2020/04/fireteam-modern-2020-edition-by-rory.html)

### 5 Parsecs From Home
Originally Ivan Sorensen / Nordic Weasel Games; full retail edition now published by Modiphius (which acquired the IP alongside Five Leagues in June 2023).
*A ragtag sci-fi crew procedurally generates its own missions, travels, and campaign story, solo or co-op, from a handful of miniatures.*
**Why it matches:** persistent small crew, procedural mission generation, solo/co-op-first design — directly relevant to the campaign-layer and solo/co-op open questions, even though the setting is sci-fi rather than strictly "modern."
**The mechanic:** `[CONSENSUS]` procedurally generated encounters, loot, and crew events from tables, with **5 difficulty settings** plus escalating "Black Zone"/"Red Zone" job tiers layered on top of the base crew-progression loop.
**Availability:** in print + PDF, Modiphius / Wargame Vault / major retailers — currently in active print.
**Source:** [Modiphius product page](https://modiphius.net/en-us/products/five-parsecs-from-home) · [Wargame Vault listing](https://www.wargamevault.com/en/product/358938/five-parsecs-from-home-solo-adventure-wargaming)

### Two Hour Wargames (Chain Reaction engine — 5150, All Things Zombie, etc.)
Two Hour Wargames · Ed Teixeira · the shared **Chain Reaction** engine has run continuously since the mid-2000s (current edition sold as "Chain Reaction 2023/2026").
*A reusable core engine (5150 = sci-fi warband, All Things Zombie = zombie survival, and others) built explicitly for solo and co-op play.*
**Why it matches:** this is the one place in the sweep where the fog-of-war/detection mechanic Settlements keeps circling back to has a real, load-bearing published implementation.
**The mechanic:** `[FACT — described directly by a 4chan-hosted rules PDF plus a blog reaction-test walkthrough, not the full rulebook itself]` **PEFs (Possible Enemy Forces)** — markers placed on the table at scenario start that move via a 2d6 **PEF Movement Table**, activating highest-to-lowest, and only convert to an actual enemy group (or a false alarm, or "something is out there") when a Reaction Test resolves them. It's a full uncertainty-of-contact system, not a single spotting roll.
**Availability:** in print + PDF, sold directly by Two Hour Wargames and via Wargame Vault; a free rules tier exists.
**Source:** [Two Hour Wargames — Chain Reaction 2023](https://www.twohourwargames.com/products/chain-reaction-2023) · [Chain Reaction 3.0 PDF excerpt](https://i.4pcdn.org/tg/1404020348575.pdf)

### Wreck Age
Hyacinth Games · 2nd edition ~2018.
*A post-collapse crew skirmish game hybridised with an RPG — a campaign "Narrator" plans encounters, and resource management drives the campaign layer.*
**Why it matches:** the Narrator-as-campaign-referee structure and resource-driven campaign play are close to a base-building crew campaign in spirit.
**The mechanic:** `[CONSENSUS]` the game foregrounds the **group**, not the individual model, as the unit of campaign investment — a different answer to "what persists" than the per-model advancement most of this genre defaults to.
**Availability:** `[NOT FOUND]` — website and social accounts exist but no confirmed activity in 2024–2026 was found in this sweep; **confirm the company is still trading before buying.**
**Source:** [wreck-age.net store](http://www.wreck-age.net/index.php/online-store) · [Must Contain Minis — 2nd Edition initial thoughts](https://mustcontainminis.com/2018/02/wreck-age-2nd-edition-hyacinth-games-initial-thoughts.html)

### Maelstrom's Edge
Spiral Arm Studios · V2 ruleset current, originally Kickstarted ~2015.
*A galaxy-devouring anomaly (the Maelstrom) forces five factions — including corporate drone/warmech operators and exosuit-clad raiders — into squad-based combat over the worlds it hasn't yet destroyed.*
**Why it matches:** not the setting (far-future, explicitly space-opera) but two factions bring exactly the hardware Settlements cares about — the **Epirian Foundation** runs "a wide array of powerful robotic drones and warmechs," and the **Artarian Remnant** is "elite exosuit-clad" raiders.
**The mechanic:** `[CONSENSUS]` squad-based play built around **suppression + flanking as two explicitly separate jobs** — one unit's role is to pin, a different unit's role is to punish the pin.
**Availability:** in print, sold direct by Spiral Arm Studios + Wayland Games / Noble Knight.
**Source:** [Maelstrom's Edge official site](https://www.maelstromsedge.com/medge/) · [BGG entry](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/211191/maelstroms-edge-battle-for-zycanthus)

### Dark Age
CMON · originally Dark Age Games, later CMON Global Limited.
*Seven factions fight for survival on the shattered world of Samaria — humans, their own monstrous creations, and the planet itself.*
**Why it matches:** post-apocalyptic gang-vs-gang skirmish at crew scale, D20-driven.
**The mechanic:** `[NOT FOUND]` — no specific mechanic verified this run beyond the D20 core; tone leans further toward monster/fantasy hybrids (mutants, alien invaders) than grounded mil-tech, which is the actual reason this sits in "worth a look" rather than "strong match."
**Availability:** in print, active releases confirmed as recently as Jan 2026 — **but publisher CMON reported a ~$20M loss in 2025 and paused new crowdfunding until "trade conditions stabilize,"** with a stated intent to resume in H2 2026. Confirm current support before committing.
**Source:** [CMON — Dark Age product line](https://www.cmon.com/product-line/dark-age/) · [Wargamer — CMON 2026 financial report](https://www.wargamer.com/board-games/cmon-2026-report)

### This Is Not a Test
Originally Joseph McGuire (Kickstarted, launched 2015); current 2nd edition published by Black Site Studio.
*A 28mm post-apocalyptic skirmish set in the "Tri-State Wasteland" (a ruined Maryland/Pennsylvania/West Virginia).*
**Why it matches:** already on our internal radar — [[Wargaming Research Hub]]'s "To read next" list and [[Library Coverage]] both flag this alongside [[Zona Alfa]] as an uncaptured post-apoc want, and it remains uncaptured. Flagging here mainly to confirm it's still obtainable and to record that a **2nd edition exists**, which the earlier note didn't have.
**The mechanic:** `[NOT FOUND]` — not independently re-verified this run beyond confirming the 2nd edition's existence.
**Availability:** 2nd edition core rulebook currently sold direct by Black Site Studio.
**Source:** [Black Site Studio — 2nd Edition core rulebook](https://blacksitestudio.com/products/this-is-not-a-test-core-rulebook-2nd-edition) · [BGG entry](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/146387/not-test-post-apocalyptic-skirmish-rules)

## ⚠️ Probably not, and why

### Rogue Stars
Osprey Wargames #17 · Andrea Sfiligoi (designer of [[Song of Blades and Heroes]]) · 20 Dec 2016.
*Crews of 4–6 — bounty hunters, pirates, smugglers — skirmish across a science-fiction frontier underworld.*
**Why it ranks down:** the setting is explicitly fringe-frontier space opera (pirates, bounty hunters, alien atmospheres), which is exactly the tone the profile asks us to exclude — this is a strong *system* from a designer whose other work we already use, in the wrong *setting* for this project.
**Source:** [Osprey product page](https://www.ospreypublishing.com/us/rogue-stars-9781472810779/)

### Punkapocalyptic
Bad Roll Games (Spain) · 30mm.
*Small warbands (5–12 models) of mutants, junkers and gangs brawl in a "punk, irreverent... black humor" post-apocalypse.*
**Why it ranks down:** `[CONSENSUS — this is the reviewers' own characterisation, not my judgment]` the tone is played for slapstick/black comedy, which cuts against Settlements' "grounded, gritty, lethal" mandate even though the scale and premise (small post-apoc warbands) otherwise fit well.
**Source:** [Punkapocalyptic official site](https://punkapocalyptic.com/en/) · [terrainandminis roundup](https://terrainandminis.com/en/blog/best-post-apocalyptic-miniatures-games.html)

### Warfighter
Dan Verssen Games.
*Solo/co-op card-driven special-forces mission game — Middle East insurgents, jungle drug cartels, and expansions like the Kandahar module.*
**Why it ranks down:** **this is not a miniatures game.** It's card- and standee-driven, no models on a table. Setting and mission structure are on-genre, but the physical format is the wrong medium for what this sweep is sourcing.
**Source:** [BGG — Warfighter: Modern Expansion #50 Kandahar](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/286366/warfighter-modern-expansion-50-kandahar)

### DANGER CLOSE
Dice Goblin Games, itch.io · active WIP (v0.99 as of this search).
*Squad-based tactical combat: five Troopers push through hostile Sectors toward objectives.*
**Why it ranks down:** **this is a TTRPG, not a miniatures wargame — the same trap that snagged Judge Dredd last run.** The author's own description states the design goal directly: *"made with the aim to replicate the dramatic vignettes that might occur in tabletop war games, **without all the hassle, bookkeeping, investment and duration of said games**."* Itch.io tags it `Role Playing`, and it's explicitly setting-agnostic (its own pitch reskins the same engine as 40k Imperial Guard, Halo ODSTs, or Star Wars clones). Worth a note only because its **campaign-structure options** (Tide of War / Tree Campaigns / Grand Campaigns, each a different shape of mission-linking) are a clean menu of campaign architectures if Settlements' campaign layer ever wants to compare shapes — but it is not itself a candidate for miniatures-rules research.
**Source:** [DANGER CLOSE — itch.io](https://dicegoblingames.itch.io/danger-close-tactical-skirmish-ttrpg)

## 🔍 Searched and found nothing

**A real, mechanised electronic-warfare / jamming / signal-denial layer does not appear to exist in any published skirmish miniatures game found this run.** Searches specifically for "jamming," "counter-UAS," and "electronic warfare" in a wargame-rules context returned real-world defense literature and general "how do we handle drones on the table" hobbyist forum threads, but no rulebook that models *denying* a drone or network connection as its own mechanic — as opposed to just shooting the drone down. Drones do appear as **flavour or a unit type** in several games above (Maelstrom's Edge's Epirian robotic drones/warmechs, Combat Zone's "machines" faction) but none of them model jamming or spoofing as a distinct sub-system. If Settlements builds a real EW layer, it will likely be inventing structure the hobby hasn't shipped yet, rather than adapting an existing one — genuinely useful to know before spending design time hunting for a precedent that isn't there.
