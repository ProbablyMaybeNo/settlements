# 🎲 Warhammer 40,000

> [!abstract] In one breath
> Games Workshop ran the *"should wargear cost points?"* experiment for **25 years in public**, and the answer arrived in June 2026. The arc — and the reason each step was taken — is the most decision-relevant case study in the corpus for any game shipping a player-built armoury.

## The full arc

**Type:** Costing · **Take:** 📎 reference

**[FACT]** Per-item costs (3rd–7th) → **dual system** (8th) → **quiet deletion** (9th) → **all wargear free** (10th) → **June 2026, 11th edition partially reversed it**: gear is free by default and priced *only* where one option provably dominates, **with the cost rebased out of the chassis so default loadouts stay points-neutral.**

**GW's stated reason for going free was never fairness — it was overhead versus yield.** Robin Cruddace: per-item costs *"added to the complexity of working out your army, **for little gain regarding the actual output of the unit on the battlefield**."* **[FACT]**

> **The question to interrogate is not "can we cost gear correctly?" but "is the cost of costing it worth what it buys?"**

**And per-item costing collapses on its own terms.** In one 4th-edition codex a heavy bolter cost **+5 on a Tactical Squad and +15 on a Command Squad** — **a 3× spread on identical wargear**, because the number was really pricing the *slot*, not the gun. **[FACT]**

> **Once an atom needs context-dependent prices, it has stopped being an atom — and has kept all of the bookkeeping.**

**For Settlements.** The 11th-edition settlement is the one worth studying, because it's the compromise a company reached after trying both extremes: **free by default, priced at proven outliers, rebased so the default costs the same.** That is a real option for [[Weapons]] characteristics and is written up as a live proposal in `docs/POINTS-RESEARCH.md` §8.3b. Compare [[Kill Team#The three-philosophy experiment]] — the same company, the same question, three answers in six years at a smaller scale.

## The hidden price of free wargear

**Type:** Costing · **Take:** ⚠️ the sharpest lesson in the corpus

**Free wargear only holds if the options are genuine sidegrades**, which GW said explicitly. **11th's partial reversal is the admission that some weren't.** **[FACT]**

And here is what it costs to make them sidegrades:

> **To make N options cost the same, you must make them *worth* the same — so you homogenise the profiles.** GW's Death Guard flails, cleavers and maces all collapsed into **one identical weapon.** **[FACT]**

> **You cannot escape paying for differentiation. Either you pay in points arithmetic, or you pay in flavour.**

**For Settlements — this is the decisive one.** Our pillar is a **DIY armoury where weapons are built, not bought off a shelf** ([[Weapons]]). For a game with that pillar, **flavour is the expensive currency** — which argues for keeping priced characteristics and importing only the *points stepper* and the principle **"cost the decision, not the item."**

**And there's a testable audit in here we can run today:** any characteristic in [[Weapons]] that is a straight **upgrade** rather than a **sidegrade** must be priced or gated. That's an empirical claim about our own catalogue, not an assumption — and it's exactly the check [[Kill Team#If you make it free, make it mandatory]] independently arrives at.

## Army composition, briefly

**[FACT/CONSENSUS]** The Force Organisation Chart lineage (compulsory Troops, capped Elites/Heavy Support) is the ancestor of every slot-based composition rule in this vault, including our own **rank pyramid**. The relevant warning is [[Warmachine and Hordes#The category trap]]: a requirement written in terms of a category assumes its members are interchangeable. Our pyramid is safe **because rank *is* the cost tier** — a rule saying "you must take two *ranged* specialists" would not be.
