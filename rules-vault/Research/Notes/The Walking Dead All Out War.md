# 🎲 The Walking Dead: All Out War

> [!abstract] In one breath
> One of the very few games that **publishes its custom-character costing formula** — and then prints, in the same book, an admission that it doesn't quite work. Its other standout is the best neutral-threat design found anywhere: **walkers are costed against the *game*, not against either player, and both players get to aim them.**

## The published formula, and its confession

**Type:** Costing · **Take:** ⭐ steal the honesty

Characteristic dice are priced **X / Y / Z** — pay X for your first die in that characteristic, Y each for a second, Z each for a third **[FACT]**:

| Die tier | Melee | Shoot | Defense |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best (blue) | 10 / 12 / **16** | 12 / 15 / **20** | 7 / 10 / 12 |
| Mid (white) | 5 / 6 / 7 | 6 / 7 / **8** | 3 / 4 / 5 |
| Worst (red) | 3 / 3 / 5 | 3 / 4 / 6 | 2 / 2 / 3 |

Confirmed by the book's own worked example: *"two white dice and one blue… for the Shoot value that would be a whopping total of 36 points – 8+8+20."* Plus Nerve (Low +0 / Medium +5 / High +15), Health (3 = +0 rising to 8 = +20), Pack Slots (1 = +0 to 4 = +5), up to two Special Rules (2–20 pts) and one Leader Ability (5–10 pts).

**And then the designers admit, in print, that it doesn't quite work** **[FACT — verbatim]**:

> *"You'll notice that some of the Survivors in the game work out slightly different to the points values here would indicate. **As there's no way of accounting for every possible combination of characteristic and ability, it's hard to truly gauge the effectiveness of a custom character, so these rules include a slight premium.** As a result, it's best to only use custom characters in friendly games in which your opponent agrees to their use."*

> **This is the most honest statement of the atomic-costing problem found anywhere in the research** — and the mitigation is instructive: **ship the formula, add a deliberate premium for the combinations you can't price, and quarantine the whole system to friendly play.** **[INFERENCE]**

**For Settlements — read this carefully, because it's our exact situation.** We have a player-built [[Weapons]] system and a global points engine, which means we *are* shipping a construction kit. Three transferable moves:

1. **The escalating per-die ladder** (10 / 12 / 16 for successive dice in one characteristic) is a **super-linear price on stacking the same axis** — the same instinct as [[One Page Rules#The stat ladder doubles at the top]] and [[Games Workshop published formulas#Rogue Trader, 1987]]. Three systems, same fix.
2. **A stated premium is a legitimate design tool.** If the combination space can't be priced exactly, charge a margin and *say so*.
3. **Quarantine is a real option.** "Custom builds in friendly play, published profiles in matched play" is a shipped answer to a problem we currently plan to solve entirely with maths.

## Re-pricing veterans

**Type:** Campaign · **Take:** ⚙️ adapt — and note the disagreement

> *"Any character that was not removed as a casualty during the game receives an Experience bonus of one red die, **and increases their points value by 3 points**."* **[FACT]**

**The exact opposite position to [[Trench Crusade#Cap the veterans, don't tax them]]**, which never re-prices a veteran at all. Two games in the same genre, released within two years, taking opposite positions on the exact fork Settlements faces. **[INFERENCE] Neither is reported as broken.**

**For Settlements.** Our current design taxes advances (+2 points each), which puts us on the TWD side of the fork. **The finding is not "one of these is right" — it's "pick one and let the other axis run."** Half of each gives you veterans that are both expensive *and* capped, which is how a campaign roster stops being fun.

## The neutral threat

**Type:** Scenario · **Take:** ⭐ steal — genuinely elegant

> *"You will need a number of Walkers chosen to the same points limit as the game, rounding up. **Walkers are 15 points each**, so in a 50-point game you would need 4 Walkers, while in a 300-point game you would need 20."* **[FACT]**

Walkers are fully neutral — *"enemy… refers to any model that is not part of your own Survivor group, be it one of your opponent's Survivors or one of the Walkers"* — and **both players get to aim them**: event cards instruct *each* player to move N Walkers in a direction of their choice, alternating.

**Why it works.** **[INFERENCE]** The third-party threat **scales linearly with the size of the fight, so a bigger game is not a safer game** — and **nobody pays for it out of their own list.** The threat is a property of the scenario, not a purchase. And making it *steerable by both sides* turns it from atmosphere into a tactical resource: the walkers are a weapon neither player owns.

**For Settlements.** This is the mechanical answer to the [[Ideas Inbox]] want for boards that *"feel alive"* — a pack of wolves, a rival scavenger band, an automated turret still running on old power. **Cost the threat against the game's Crew Rating cap, not against either crew**, and let both players push it. It composes directly with [[Events]] (already drafted, battlefield-only) and [[Scenarios]], and it's a way to make a territory feel dangerous without either player buying the danger.
