# 🎲 Malifaux

> [!abstract] In one breath
> A card-driven gothic skirmish game whose **list-building** is far more interesting than its costing. Deliberately coarse atoms (50 Soulstones, models 2–15 SS), a **free Leader**, leftover budget that converts into an in-game resource, and the single best small idea in the whole research corpus: **the +1 out-of-keyword hiring tax**.

> Correction to a common belief: **M3E did *not* remove upgrade-buying.** Upgrade Cards exist in M3E, carry Costs, and are purchased during hiring. What changed was their **scope and volume**. **[FACT — verified against the Rules Manual]**

---
## The keyword tax

**Type:** Faction · **Take:** ⭐ steal — top of the list

> *"A player may hire any models that share one or more Keywords with their Leader… Additionally, a player may hire any models that belong to their declared Faction, though **models that do not share a Keyword with their Crew's Leader have their Cost increased by +1 during hiring**. The exception to this rule are models with the **Versatile** Characteristic, which do not have their Cost increased."* **[FACT]**

**One flat, tiny, universally-applied modifier makes thematic lists cheaper than optimised ones — without banning anything, and without a second price list.** Plus a named exemption (`Versatile`) so the designer can hand-pick which units are *meant* to travel between crews.

**Why it works.** It's a soft gradient where most games use a hard wall. Nobody is told "you may not take that"; they're told it costs one more. The thematic list wins on efficiency, the mercenary list wins on answers, and both are legal. It is the cheapest possible implementation of the "colour wheel" faction-identity principle.

**For Settlements.** Directly applicable to [[Factions]], and it composes with — rather than replaces — [[BLKOUT#Force Rules — one rule, one playstyle]]. The `Versatile` exemption is the half people forget: **without a designer-controlled escape hatch, a tax on breadth becomes a tax on interesting lists.**

## The free leader

**Type:** List · **Take:** ⭐ steal

> *"Each player must hire their chosen Leader. However, **that Leader's Cost is treated as 0** when hiring."* **[FACT]**

Totems are conditionally free too: *"If this Master is a Crew's Leader, the Totem's Cost is considered to be 0 during hiring."*

**Why it works.** The budget gets spent only on **real choices**. A mandatory purchase priced at its "true" value is just a smaller budget with extra arithmetic — and it makes every Leader's price a balance problem you didn't need to have.

**For Settlements.** Worth weighing against our locked rank-body costs (Match Play 65/95/165/245). We charge for the Leader; Malifaux doesn't. Neither is wrong — but if a Leader is **mandatory**, its price is doing no gating work, only subtraction. See [[List Building]].

## Leftover budget becomes a capped resource

**Type:** List · **Take:** ⭐ steal

> *"Any unspent points become Soulstones in your Crew's Soulstone Pool. A Crew's Soulstone Pool **cannot exceed 10 Soulstones** during hiring."* **[FACT]**

**Why it works.** Two problems solved with one line. Nobody is punished for a list that doesn't spend to the last point — the remainder converts into something useful — and because the conversion is **capped**, deliberately under-building to hoard resources is bounded and can't become a strategy.

**For Settlements.** A live option for [[List Building]]: unspent Credits at a Match Play 1000 cap currently just evaporate. Converting them into a small, capped in-game resource would remove the "shave the list to hit exactly 1000" chore, which is pure friction.

## One upgrade at hiring

**Type:** List · **Take:** ⭐ steal

> *"During hiring, **any model can pay for and Attach a single Upgrade**. This limit only applies during hiring; **there is no limit** to the number of Upgrades a model may Attach during gameplay."* **[FACT]**

Plus a tiny constraint vocabulary printed on the cards: `Restricted (Name)`, `Special (Name)` (cannot be hired, only attached in-game), `Plentiful (X)`.

**Why it works.** The combinatorial explosion that makes upgrades unbalanceable happens **at list-building**, where a player can search the whole space offline. In play, upgrades are gated by tempo and circumstance. **Cap where the search is free; open up where the game is already the constraint.**

**For Settlements.** Relevant to any equipment-stacking in [[List Building]], and the three-word constraint vocabulary is worth copying wholesale — it's less text than a paragraph of restrictions.

## Coarse atoms and fractional pricing

**Type:** Costing · **Take:** 📎 reference

50 Soulstones total, models 2–15 SS — **very coarse atoms**, and the game is fine. Two prices are expressed as **fractions of game size** rather than as numbers **[FACT]**: extra Masters' combined cost must be *"less than half this encounter's size,"* and each costs *"the cost of 1 additional Soulstone to hire."*

**For Settlements.** A price expressed as a fraction of the budget auto-scales across our Campaign Start 500 / Match Play 1000 / pitched 1500 tiers, where a flat number doesn't. Compare [[Trench Crusade#Caps keyed to force value]].

## The upgrade failure mode

**Type:** Campaign · **Take:** ⚠️ instructive failure

The designers on why Emissary upgrades were removed **[CONSENSUS — quote surfaced via search, original URL unconfirmed; attribute cautiously]**: they *"were generally used as patches to sub-par Masters and were either not impressive enough to see the table or so strong that they became mandatory hires,"* and removing them let designers *"focus on making each Emissary good on its own merits."*

**This is the atomic-costing failure mode in its purest form: a costed option collapses into "never taken" or "auto-taken," and the band between is narrow.** **[INFERENCE]**

**And the deeper point:** an upgrade written to *patch a weak unit* lands on auto-take **by construction** — the patch is worth more than its price by design intent. M3E's surface fix was structural (one upgrade per model at hiring); the real fix was to **fold auto-take content into the base card** so it stops being a purchase decision at all.

**For Settlements.** A standing test for [[Weapons]] and [[Skill Paths]]: *is this option a patch?* If it exists to make something weak playable, it should be in the base profile, not on the price list.
