# 🎲 Mad Dogs with Guns

> [!abstract] In one breath
> A **Prohibition-era gangster skirmish game** in the Necromunda/Mordheim warband-campaign tradition. The core system is *"decent and basic"* — **what makes it sing are the campaign rules**, and specifically an escalating bribery economy that is the closest published analogue to what the Settlements territory layer wants to be.

## Turf, and the one named site

**Type:** Settlement · **Take:** ⭐ steal

Gangs own and fight over **different kinds of turf** — speakeasies, gambling dens, brothels. **Each type has several basic sites** that give different bonuses, **plus a single unique, named one** that gives that bonus *plus* extras.

**Why it works.** The generic tier makes the territory map *fillable* — there are always more speakeasies — while the named site makes one specific square on the map **worth fighting a campaign over**. It's a cheap way to create landmarks without designing a bespoke rule for every location. And because the named site is a superset of the generic one, a player already knows how it works the first time they take it.

**For Settlements.** Directly applicable to [[Territory]] and the [[Ideas Inbox]] want that each territory carries a **type** which drives its terrain list and objectives. **The generic/named split is the piece to steal**: several generic territories per type, each giving the type's benefit, and **one named landmark per type** giving that benefit plus something unique. It also gives [[Scenarios]] a natural escalation — the named site is the one worth a raid.

## Bribery, and the price of corruption

**Type:** Campaign · **Take:** ⭐ steal — the standout mechanic

Gangs can spend their income **bribing the press, police, judges and politicians** — subverting the levers of society to their ends. Key figures in your pocket give you **different sorts of considerations**.

**And here is the mechanic that makes it sing:**

> **As different gangs compete to bribe these authority figures, the crooked SOBs get greedier — the price of bribes goes up over the course of the campaign.**

**Why it works.** It is a **shared, contested resource that self-inflates.** Three things fall out of one rule:

1. **Contested without a battle.** Two gangs can fight over the same judge across the whole campaign without a model ever being placed.
2. **A natural money sink that scales.** The escalating price soaks up the income that would otherwise become the snowball — and it soaks up *more* from whoever has the most, because they're the one who keeps bidding.
3. **Early advantage doesn't compound.** Getting the police chief first is worth something, but it *raises the price of everything else*, including for you.

**[INFERENCE]** That third point is the real prize: it is an **anti-snowball valve disguised as flavour.** Most catch-up mechanics feel like charity. This one feels like corruption working exactly the way corruption works.

**For Settlements.** We have a near-future 2051 America in a second civil war — the analogues write themselves: **local militia commanders, corporate security contractors, regional administrators, black-market brokers, the remnants of federal authority.** A standing favour bought with [[Economy|Credits]] that costs more every time anyone in the campaign buys one. It would sit in [[Downtime]] as a spend option, pay out in [[Territory]] or [[Diplomacy]] (currently one of three unfinished notes), and give the campaign a political layer that isn't a battle.

**The open questions to settle in a real read:** does the price rise globally or per-figure? Does a bribe expire? Can a rival *outbid* you for a figure you already own — and if so, does that create a bidding war that drains everyone, or just the two richest?
