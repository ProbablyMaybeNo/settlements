# 🎲 Frostgrave & Stargrave

> [!abstract] In one breath
> Warband campaign games whose headline structural finding is that **gold and experience are two economies with no exchange rate between them.** Gold buys **bodies and items**. XP buys **stats, spells and powers**. Neither converts, in either direction — the same principle Settlements locked independently.

## Two economies, no exchange rate

**Type:** Campaign · **Take:** ⭐ steal — already ours, now corroborated

**Gold buys bodies and items. Experience buys stats, spells and powers.** **[FACT]** There is no way to buy a stat with gold and no way to buy a soldier with XP.

The wizard/captain is **free**, their gear is **free**, and spells and powers are **free** — limited by slots and count, not by price. The budget covers only the apprentice (100gc) and the soldiers.

**Why it works.** It removes the arbitrage that breaks most point-buy campaign games. If stats can be bought with the same currency as bodies, a player can always convert quantity into quality (or the reverse) at whatever rate the designer got slightly wrong — and they will find that rate. **Two hard-separated currencies means there's no rate to get wrong.**

**For Settlements.** This is our locked principle — *points buy bodies and guns; stats and skills are free* ([[List Building]], `POINTS-DECISIONS` D22/D27) — and it's worth noting that **a designer with ten years of shipped campaign games arrived at the same conclusion independently.** Same finding again in [[Last Days Zombie Apocalypse]] (SP vs XP, never converting). Three systems, same answer.

## Two constraints that take turns binding

**Type:** List · **Take:** ⭐ steal — a genuinely elegant piece of design

**[INFERENCE, from published numbers]** 400gc minus the 100gc apprentice leaves **300gc for 8 soldiers — 37.5 average.** You **cannot** fill your 4 specialist slots at creation: 4 × 125 = 500gc on its own. The natural level-0 build is 100 + (4 × 75) + 4 free = **exactly 400**.

> **The specialist cap is not the binding constraint at creation — the budget is. The cap only starts biting several games into the campaign, once treasure accumulates.**

**Why it works.** Two constraints that take turns being the real one gives you a campaign that changes shape over time **without a single rule changing.** Early games are about affording anything; late games are about the ceiling. That's a progression arc for free.

**For Settlements.** Directly relevant to the **Campaign Start 500** vs **Match Play 1000** split. At 500 the budget binds; at 1000 the rank pyramid should. **If both bind at once, you've collapsed the arc — check that the pyramid isn't the binding constraint at 500.** See [[List Building]] and [[Campaign]].

## The published price ladders

**Type:** Costing · **Take:** 📎 reference

Note how **few price points exist** **[FACT]**. Frostgrave: **Free / 10 / 50 / 75 / 100 / 125**. Stargrave: **Free / 10 / 20 / 50 / 75 / 100 / 150**.

| Frostgrave soldier | Cost | | Stargrave soldier | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thug, Thief | **Free** | | Recruit, Runner | **Free** |
| War Hound | 10 | | Guard Dog | 10 |
| Infantryman | 50 | | Hacker, Chiseler | 20 |
| Man-at-Arms, Apothecary | 75 | | Sentry, Trooper | 50 |
| Archer, Crossbowman | 75 *(specialist)* | | Codebreaker, Commando | 75 *(specialist)* |
| Treasure Hunter, Tracker | 100 *(specialist)* | | Medic | 100 |
| Knight, Templar, Ranger, Marksman | 125 *(specialist)* | | Sniper, Grenadier, Gunner | 100 *(specialist)* |
| | | | Armoured Trooper | 150 *(specialist)* |

**[INFERENCE]** The implied curve is legible even with no formula published: **Free** = one good stat and nothing else · **50** = +3 Fight · **75** = +3 Fight plus protection, a second good stat, or a utility role · **100** = two good stats · **125** = +4 Fight or a broad three-stat profile. Roughly **25gc per meaningful stat step**, with a deliberately flat but playable free tier.

**The free tier is the interesting part.** A **genuinely free, genuinely playable** body means a player can always field a full warband no matter how badly the campaign has gone. That is a floor under the death spiral that costs nothing to implement. Compare [[Necromunda and Mordheim#Lasting injuries]], where the absence of such a floor is the known flaw.

**[NOT FOUND — a firm negative, not a gap.]** No derivation is published. The only justification given is in-fiction (costs are "retainers"; specialists *"require larger retainers and positions of authority"*). The "Why A Second Edition?" designer essay discusses spell rebalancing in detail — ~20 of 80 spells were *"too weak or too situational"* — and **never mentions soldier pricing.** McCullough also states the game *"is less concerned with being a tactical exercise"* than with *"a shared sense of fun and adventure."*
