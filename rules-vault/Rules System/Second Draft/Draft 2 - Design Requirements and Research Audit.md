# Draft 2 Design Requirements and Research Audit

The accompanying [[Settlements Rules System Version 2]] turns Ross's five tenets and follow-up answers into a complete proposed rules manuscript. This audit records what those requirements mean in play, which research informed the choices, what changed from the older drafts, and what still needs evidence. Research is a source of design options, not permission to call an untested rule balanced.

## Authority and scope

Ross explicitly authorised completing missing rules and changing existing Draft 2 rules where necessary. That supersedes the earlier instruction to leave undecided sections blank for this drafting task. His latest brief is the design authority; the previous Draft 2 supplies a starting engine and vocabulary, and Draft 1 supplies historical examples. Neither an external game's mechanic nor an assistant's new number is a user-approved ruling merely because it appears here.

**Direct requirements:** realistic but cinematic civilians; simple procedures with tactical depth; dangerous actions tested; objective-based battles; 180-degree vision; interactive terrain and hacking; drones; alternating activations and per-action reactions (clarified October 18); Down, rescue, loot, capture, unconsciousness after two complete rounds; persistence without a campaign; agreed Crew Rating; structures, Materials, allocated Power and modest worker benefits; 3 × 3 feet, six rounds, roughly 5–10 starting fighters, and a roughly one-hour familiar-play target.

**Design proposals:** exact action restrictions, numerical limits, prices, recruitment, the 40-skill selection, settlement outputs, XP thresholds, injury probabilities, capture return time, and every newly completed procedure. They are playable defaults awaiting review and playtesting, not claims that Ross dictated those details.

**Deliberately deferred content:** fiction, setting exposition, factions, authored mission stories and maps, and a linked campaign framework. Persistent settlement play, progression, scenario construction procedures, and injury rules are included because those are mechanics needed to play the requested game.

The thirteen previous Second Draft notes were preserved before editing in `Rules System/Archived/Draft 2 before complete rulebook - 2026-10-17/`. The new manuscript is the working authority; the old master becomes a redirect. Historical simulations remain historical.

## Research coverage and evidence limits

The curated Research folder contained **41 Markdown files** at the start: 33 game/design notes and eight indexes, templates or synthesis notes. The 33 game notes were reviewed at the sections relevant to this game; shorter entries were read in full, longer entries by their core procedure, settlement, progression, interaction and critique sections. This was a substantive design audit, not a claim that every paragraph of every linked source rulebook was read anew.

The Rule of Carnage design reference was read in full. The saved corpus contains **83 distinct videos and 688 extracted findings**. Sixteen relevant episodes received a closer review through their audited findings; six key arguments were checked directly against timestamped transcript passages. The other videos remain searchable background coverage, not newly revalidated transcripts in this pass. No new transcript download or outside rules-edition verification was needed for this local synthesis.

The workspace keeps the pre-edit Research inventory with hashes, a game-note reading log, and pre-edit Draft 2 hashes under `docs/draft2-completion-2026-10-17/`. These document scope and preserve reproducibility; a hash is not proof that a claim in a note is correct.

### Source quality findings

1. **Draft 1 assumptions are embedded in research commentary.** References to “our d10,” 7+, old prices, campaign-only persistence and adopted structures describe that older draft. They cannot govern this d20 manuscript.
2. **A source description and a recommendation are different evidence.** “Steal,” star ratings, “best,” and “what it gets wrong” are curatorial judgments. The new text uses their reasoning where relevant, without treating them as measurements.
3. **Balance claims do not transfer between engines.** Old terrain win-rate swings, million-game totals, atom prices and community campaign simulations have different conditions. They identify risks; they do not validate new prices, injury rates, READY timing or a sixty-minute game.
4. **Some precedent is edition-sensitive or inferred.** Inferred formulas in costing notes and partial source captures must remain labelled. No claim here certifies the latest commercial edition's exact rules.
5. **The shortlist contains choices now rejected by the brief.** Losing a structure deleting already-owned assets is too punitive for this persistent two-player core. Free veterans above a fielded cap also conflict with the agreed Crew Rating. A larger menu and repriced veterans can coexist; they are not mutually exclusive choices.
6. **Community campaign fixes have a different authority.** Necromunda homebrew and forum observations are useful failure reports, not official rules or controlled experiments. They do not justify a mandatory organiser or access to a large player pool.
7. **Transcripts are imperfect text.** Automated captions can distort names and terms. Use nearby context and the linked video when exact wording matters. The recommendations below are paraphrases, not attributed verbatim quotations.

## Rule of Carnage findings that changed this draft

Read alongside [[Rule of Carnage Skirmish Game Design Reference]], [[Rule of Carnage Episode Notes]] and [[Rule of Carnage Episode Index]]. The source column links to the relevant point in the original video; episode labels below describe the topic. “Direct check” marks the six transcript passages revisited in this pass.

| Topic and source | Argument relevant to Settlements | Application and tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Core loop](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vtqS8bLGJ3c&t=610s) | Identify what players repeatedly do and how that creates the intended experience. | Each activation combines one Move and one Core in either order; reactions address individual actions. Settlement management sits outside that loop. Two slots still need tracking. |
| [Damage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WFrPm84xZPU&t=1023s) | A casualty state needs decisions and payoff, not simply a delay before removal. | Down creates rescue, self-aid, theft and capture; Unconscious is a clear endpoint. Extra bodies remain on the board. |
| [Game length](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UUgKFJ1ru7k&t=153s) | Duration is produced by actions and procedures, not only a round cap. | Six rounds plus one own attack, bounded reactions and small initial crews. Sixty minutes remains a test target, especially at ten fighters. |
| [Scenarios](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xMzPFNzemr4&t=1256s) | Killing already changes control; scoring it again can distort objectives. | No generic kill points; multiple nonlethal scoring procedures and useful secondary tasks. Rescue remains optional, since it depends on casualties. |
| [Activation advantage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m6b3SgJSPaM&t=755s) | Unequal unit counts create timing advantages beyond raw power. | Balancing Passes removed by Ross on 2026-10-19; no replacement balancing mechanic. Test 5 against 10. |
| [Shooting and terrain](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oXg_k-hnbiI&t=1656s) — direct check | Terrain instructions and arcs matter; neither is a universal cure for gunlines. | Explicit approach routes, four interactive features and midfield scoring accompany 180-degree sight. Do not rely on the rear bonus alone. |
| [Cognitive load](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GxV9cguZ9Y0&t=216s) — direct check | Calculation and remembering states consume attention that could support decisions. | No global alert track, lingering aura layers or cybercurrency; shared statuses and named expiry. Move/Core, READY and Stress still impose real load to measure. |
| [Psychology](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T5kJZYtUlds&t=255s) — direct check | Morale can model lost control, but excessive cascades remove player agency. | Witness Stress once per round, one End Phase test, three severities, helpers and withdrawal. Fear is meaningful without repeated immediate Break chains. |
| [How to playtest](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WxmsMPyCmWk&t=490s) | Observe the intended experience and what players actually do, not only who wins. | Measure interruptions, stalled turns, meaningful terrain use, rescue decisions and boredom. Record a problem before prescribing its fix. |
| [Miniatures playtesting](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVZafm_WqkM&t=550s) | Different test stages answer different questions; blind reading exposes hidden author assumptions. | First test procedures and timing, then blind-read the manuscript/reference, then balance repeated matchups. Existing code is not a substitute. |
| [Rules bloat](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jbVEKA0tReM&t=448s) — direct check | Duplicate or rarely meaningful mechanisms can survive because they are already written. | Pin Them Down becomes core; two inventory skills merge; supernatural chains go. The reason for retaining terrain is its central purpose, not its novelty. |
| [Campaign progression](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8rLqTB5EEg4&t=2470s) — direct check | Choice breadth can itself be an advantage; progression and catch-up need deliberate limits. | Full-value advances, three skills, small winner premium, Relief floor and fixed-opponent prisoner return. A large arsenal still offers matchup choice; rating alone cannot erase it. |
| [Non-removal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RByi1Jyvy10&t=1157s) — direct check | Leaving casualties can create rescue and positional play, but needs a useful resolution. | Two-full-round rescue clock and one revival stop indefinite cycling. The reference and Down round marker must make this easy to administer. |
| [Failure](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ya77wPgJ-1A&t=748s) | Failure should change a decision without trapping play in an unproductive loop. | Failed Hack loses an action; natural 1 temporarily Freezes; manual route remains. Failed medkit consumes stock, but no extra injury table occurs mid-battle. |
| [Cursed list design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L2QHtCoEmYM&t=711s) | A match should not be predetermined by pre-game choices. | Broad basic access, objective tools available physically, limited Heavy equipment and shared scenarios. Exact costing still needs play data. |
| [Affordances](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ao1l5G6-WOE&t=558s) | What a component visibly suggests helps people understand what it can do. | Door opens, lift changes level, crane moves a marked load; feature cards use ordinary verbs. Reuse these operations for improvisation. |

**Design consequence:** the strongest recurring lesson is not “make everything smaller.” It is to spend rules and attention on decisions that express the game. Terrain, fear and rescue earn that attention. Multiple free-attack chains, overlapping aura states and a second hacking game do not.

## Risks and the next useful tests

The manuscript is complete enough to test as a whole. None of the following is evidence of a failed test; they are the most consequential uncertainties created by the design.

| Question | Small useful test | Record before changing rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can familiar play approach 60 minutes? | Six fighters each, six rounds, shared printed cards; then ten each | Actual minutes excluding setup, action count, lookups and stalled decisions. Do not infer speed from page count. |
| Does whole-fighter alternation keep both players engaged? | Same mission at 5 versus 10 fighters under equal rating, then swapped sides | Uncontested late turns, voluntary activation endings, whether READY chains stall movement. |
| Is combat bloody without deleting agency? | Cover approach, open crossing and flank on the same layout | Actions needed to Down; first-contact casualties; rescue attempts; who acts again after being Pinned. |
| Is a rear Charge too strong or melee too weak? | Symmetric front/rear approaches with basic melee and rifle crews | Chance to reach combat, Injury outcome, exposure before contact; not isolated melee averages alone. |
| Does morale add tension or a lockout? | Repeated pressure with and without support; let players wait for spent enemy actions/reactions, change LOS and create openings before standing | Break frequency, recovery timing, enemy threats still available, ranged protection, melee exposure and objective opportunity cost. Do not assume immediate recovery or inevitable re-pinning. |
| Are drones and hackers alternatives to gunfire? | Same objectives with manual controls, then one drone or hacker swap | Useful operations, action opportunity cost, device survival, remaining human objective work. |
| Does every terrain choice earn attention? | Four features, then remove the least-used one next game | Who used it, what changed, whether an alternate route existed, whether its card was reread. |
| Do capture and injury let two opponents keep playing? | Paper-run six consecutive cycles, including one severe loss | Available humans, replacement cost, prison duration, recovery overlap and minimum viable roster. |
| Does wealth or a broad arsenal overwhelm equal rating? | Same cap, novice versus veteran settlement with sides swapped | Paid advantages, objective access, roster selection burden, win pattern across multiple mission types. |
| Are catalogue prices credible? | Compare full crews across Control, Retrieve and Work | Utility and opportunity cost, not a single static objective or old atom values. |
| Can someone learn without the author? | Give new players manuscript, reference and ready-made roster | Every question and page lookup; revise unclear text before adding new mechanics. |

The first revision should respond to observed problems. If the game is slow, identify whether the cause is repeated calculations, state handling, too many bodies, excessive decisions or unclear writing before removing its defining terrain and rescue choices.

## Follow-up rulings: Move actions and Pinned — 2026-10-17

Ross clarified that each fighter has one Move Action for moving and one Core Action for shooting, interacting, hiding or another task. Standing from Pinned uses Move instead of travelling and leaves an unused Core available. He set hit modifiers against Pinned targets at −2 ranged and +2 melee, and stressed evaluating recovery through actual timing choices: players can act with other fighters while waiting for threats to expend actions/reactions or leave LOS.

These are confirmed user rulings. Their integration is recorded in [[design-006 Pinned movement and hit modifiers]]. The October 18 correction replaces action-by-action alternation with whole-fighter activations in either action order; existing specific friendly-help/Rally effects remain. Neither a modifier nor choosing a better recovery moment guarantees safety.

Collateral implementation choices remain proposals: Aim now costs Core and lasts through the following round; Mark Target and drone movement cost Core; Speed Loader, Smart and Motivate no longer exchange Move for a non-movement task. Armour Loot uses Core like other loot, and improvised Both actions must include the fighter's movement. Their revised utility and prices require playtesting; Ross did not individually specify these replacement skill effects.
