## Working rules / decisions

Settlements is a brutal skirmish wargame set in a modern civil war, where everyday citizens are forced to fight for control of their neighborhoods.

Every choice has consequences, and survival comes at a cost. Crews clash across suburbs and city streets, using highly interactive terrain to hack systems, build defenses, set traps, and tear the environment apart. In Settlements, the battlefield itself is your most powerful weapon.

Persistent crews and evolving settlements ensure every fight matters. Whether in campaign play or one-off battles, your story is shaped by the damage you take and the scars you carry forward.

### Design tenets
*Five ranked pillars, in priority order — you may only break a lower one in service of a higher one.*

1. The battlefield is the weapon  
Terrain is not just scenery—it is the primary tool for winning battles. Players are expected to interact with, modify, and exploit the environment every turn.

2. Combat is brutal and final  
Fights are fast, lethal, and decisive. Poor positioning is heavily punished, and even strong units can be taken out quickly if exposed.

3. Civilians, not soldiers  
Units represent ordinary people with limited training. Success comes from positioning, teamwork, and creativity—not superior stats.

4. Scars tell the story  
Units *and* settlements persist between games, gaining experience, lasting injuries, and history. Players shape both their fighters and their battlefield; campaign play is about survival, growth, and the world each crew carries forward.

5. Simple rules, complex outcomes  
The core rules are consistent and universal, but the situations they are applied to are varied and dynamic. Complexity comes from the environment and player decisions, not layered mechanics.

### Signature experiences

1. Terrain mastery decides the outcome  
Players win by understanding, manipulating, and controlling the battlefield—breaching walls, creating cover, setting traps, and forcing movement.

2. Every move carries real risk  
Combat is fast and lethal. Poor positioning is punished immediately, and even a single mistake can cost a unit.

3. Battles create stories, not just results  
Unexpected moments—desperate pushes, last-second survivals, catastrophic failures—emerge naturally from the interaction of players and terrain.

4. Crews become characters over time  
Units persist between games, gaining experience, suffering injuries, and developing identities shaped by what they survive.

### Out of scope — what Settlements is NOT

- Not a hero-driven game  
	There are no elite characters carrying battles. Individuals are fragile and replaceable.
- Not a stat-driven optimization game  
    Victory should not come from list building alone. Tactical play on the battlefield must outweigh pre-game optimization.
- Not designed for static battlefields  
    The system relies on players interacting with and modifying the environment. Passive terrain undermines the core experience.
- Not a rules-light narrative system  
    While the core engine is simple, the game embraces mechanical depth through terrain, conditions, and interactions.
- Not perfectly symmetrical or competitively sterile  
    The game prioritizes emergent stories and evolving crews over strict competitive balance.
- Not a game about standing still and trading shots  
    Static gunlines and passive play are intentionally weak. Movement and interaction are rewarded.
