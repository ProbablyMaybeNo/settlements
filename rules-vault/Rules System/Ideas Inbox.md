#### BIG RULES UPDATE - POST GLOBAL POINTS SYSTEM DRAFT

I want to use this section to basically try to describe the type of game I want settlers to be, the type of combsat I want players to experience, the world I want them to explore and inhabit. These ideas are not facts or auto includes. They are just my thoughts on how ideally want the Settlements game to come together and be played. 

1. Battles - I really want battles to become something more than your standard skirmish battle. I want it to feel unique and genuine. It should be highly realistic without being overly rules heavy, bloated, or a grind to get through. I want battles to feel real and therefore they should be bloody, fast paced, terrifying, and layered.
   
   - Battles should all involve interactive terrain. Raids on settlements will use the defenders settlement to set up the  board but the majority of battles won't be straight raids so we need to think of how to include interactive terrain in normal battles for control of the global map. I think this can be done in two way or maybe both. When we create the universal global map and divide it into territories each territory will be given a specific type and then based on that type users can choose or randomly select terrain from a pre-built list related to that territory type. Each of the strucutres on those lists will include an interactive mechanism. Then in addition to the players should be able to research and develop some defensive structures and add-ons which they can add to the board for territory battles things like turrets, barricades, lifts, traps, etc. Not married to this approach but I think it would work well.
     
     - In addition to interactive structures I want battles to be layered and not just focused on killing each other. Battles should be focused on a primary objective themed around advancing thec crews control of the region, wealth, and power. Things like establishing a supply line, setting up a encampment, looting a discovered equipment cache, stealing important tech from an old laboratory or lab, stealing weapons, equipment, or a vehicle from an abandoned military compound, etc. Each objective should be tied to the territory some territories casn have multiple objectives that players determine before the battle and complete the objective should provide the player with some sort of benefit or boost for example maybe the territory is an abandoned factory, the objective is to get it up and running again by connecting powerlines together and access a terminal to give it power. The player that does that takes control of the territory and is given a boost to their settlements material production or increase the amount of materials recived from scraps. 
       
       - In addition to the primary objective every location should have side quests which can vbe completed to gain additional benefits and tokens set upo around the board which can be looted and provide the looter with a one off bonus like +10 goods, +25 scraps, +1 ranged weapon, etc. Players roll on a pre-determined loot table containing a list of territory appropriate items that they roll on eachtime they loot something. this emans that playuers always havea chance of gaining something from battles even if they lose. As for secondary objectives we can think of some but they shouldn't be too complex...we'll dig into it when we draft the territories/boards.
         
         - We should include some sort of stealth and noise mechanic to increase the realism of battles. Players can sneak around the battleground looting locations and trying to complete the objective. I'm not 100% sure how this mechanic should be played out but maybe instead of move a unit can sneak which means they move at half speed but can only be seen by enemies within 12" interacting with terrain or shooting a gun or attacking someone automatically snaps them out of stealth but maybe there is a chance if they have high agility and some skills that they can sneak up behind someone and instant kill them if they pass a stealth check but if they fail then they can be attacked by the opponent before being able to attack back players can use skills or even interact with the terrain to provide increased stealth capabilities by blanketing sound or turning off the lights in a building or something like that we'd have to draft it. 
           
           - I'd like to flesh out the moral/nerve/stress/fear mechanic to make it more impactful. 
             
            - we want the boards to feel alive. Maybe for each battle or even at the beginning of each phase players roll on an events chart to see if some event happens it could be beneficial or negative or just change the battle somehow for example it starts to rain decreasing weapon range and increasing stealth, a traders caravan is passing through which the traders can interact with to purchase rare weapons or equipment, or maybe a pack of wolves appears and starts to prowl around the board atttacking the first thing that gets within 12" of them, we ashould create a list of events that could happen during battle.

- we want to make the game feel more realistic regarding the setting, the game takes place in the aftermath of a cataclysmic event. The region is filled with destroyed or abandoned locations. Equipment, weapons, armor, should feel crafted, from scavenged parts. A whole crew aren’t going to have ar 15s most of them will have gear that they made in their settlement using scraped parts. With this in mind we want to make the settlement have a larger impact on the game and specifficalky players crews. maybe equipment is mostly crafted and what can be crafted is based on structures in the settlement new or upgraded structures unlock new equipment or upgraded trader booth or market place unlock equipment that can be purchased. Maybe we link skills to structures or whatever. Players fight for new territories to unlock benefits in their settlement which provide access to new equipment, skills, strucuures, upgrades, etc.

- In the same vein I think we should try to tier weapons and equipment if we haven't already. Tier 1, 2, 3, which are unlocked by building/upgrading crafting structures. These should be the core equipment of the game however in addition to those should be non-crafted equipment that can't be made and has to be found during battles or bought from a trader. These should be rarer and better than the crafted weapons but maybe also just be different like a manufactureer beretta is going to be much better than a crafted pistol but maybe players can add conditions to the crafted pistol which they couldn't do with manufacturered weapons...same with AR 15, FALs, etc. 

- Include a fear/terror mechanism where units can "break" and become frozen in place unable to move or act until a crew member "snaps them out of it" or the controlling player rolls a certain hard to hit dice roll. "Break" is different to "Flee" or "bolt" where a unit will try to run off the board. "Break" occurs when a unit witnesses enough trauma in a single turn. Trauma can include witnessing a friendly get shot. They get shot from a out of LOS enemy. An enemy with the "Fear" trait finished a turn in their LOS or they get targeted by an enemy. Something like that. 
  
  I'd like to include more of an emphasis on morale and mental health in the game. We could include a set of "fear/terror" related affects, Break, Bolt, Freak, Fight.
  
  Break - Become frozen in the spot, must pass a specific test or base 2 base with friendly to "Snap out of it"
  
  Bolt - Heads for the nearest board edge, rolls each turn to see if they rally.
  
  Freak - Loses control and starts to attack the nearest enemy/friendly unit either in melee/shooting depending on what weapon they have.
  
  Fight - Pushed too far the unit becomes enraged and begins to move directly toward the nearest enemy in LOS ignoring cover they take the quickest most direct route no matter what.
  
  ---
  
  It would be cool to include hacking mechanics which allow players to hack into terminals and take control of the terrain, attacking opponent with turrets, setting off alarms, triggering traps, smoke, closing/locking doors. Players determine what they want to do before hacking, they select a terrain feature to trigger, then they measure the distance from their terminal, 3 12" range bands, closer is easier/farther is harder. Then they roll + skill - Difficulty. If they succeed they can trigger 1 feature in the chosen terrain.
  
  If the enemy has a hacker who is also at a terminal and within the 3 range bands they can "react" to the hack by trying to divert the hack to a different terrain, lock the hacker out by freezing their terminal for a turn, or "overload" and destroy the opponents terminal while causing the enemy to suffer a negative condition

Lets make hacking and using terrain a bigger mechanic. Maybe we say that in order to take control of the terrain a player has to hack into it. Each board set up has a pre-determined number of terminals. A player has to "hack" a terminal in order to use it to take control of a piece of terrain. BUILDER type units can build additional hacking terminals and they can build connections from an existing terminal to other pieces of terrain which then allow the players to "Control" that terrain without needing a hacker. 

This is just a thought, it's a big mechanic but it could be fun and a great way to differentiate the game.

Having a lower percentage chance to hit is fine due to the units being civilians not special forces. The average basic unit starts with 1 Dexterity which = 10% chance of hitting a unit with heavy armor in heavy cover...if my calculations are correct a super skilled shooting unit with dexterity 5 can hit a heavily armored dude in heavy cover on a 50% chance...that seems about right to me. We'll obviously include other ways to modifier a units dexterity positively turn an average joes chance of hitting a super protexted unit to 20, 30, even 40% and a unit with great dex already up to 80%. This seems like a fair and sensible stats system for a game that suggest it is "realistic, bloody, and brutal"
