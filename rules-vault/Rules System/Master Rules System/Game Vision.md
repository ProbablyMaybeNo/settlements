# Game Vision

## Summary

Settlements is a near-future, post-apocalyptic miniature skirmish game about everyday citizens fighting to survive and protect what they have built. It aims for realistic, fast-paced, bloody battles on densely packed, interactive battlefields, with simple procedures and meaningful tactical choices.

^vision-summary

Players weigh objectives, rescue, loot and survival as the situation changes. Winning matters, but so does what a crew brings home and who makes it back.

> [!info] Design direction
> This note describes the intended experience. It adds no rules or exceptions. The linked Rule Cards and Item Cards own the current procedures; testing must establish whether they deliver these goals.

## Design tenets

The five pillars remain in priority order. A lower pillar may give way only to serve a higher one.

1. **The battlefield is the weapon.** Terrain is an active part of the fight. Cover, height, routes, machinery and terminal controls should offer useful ways to change the situation beyond shooting an opponent.

2. **Combat is brutal and consequential.** Exposure and poor positioning should carry serious risks, even for experienced fighters. Armour, support and defensive skills offer protection, but casualties, Stress and rescue decisions should keep combat tense. Going Down is not automatic death.

3. **Civilians, not superheroes.** Fighters are ordinary people who can become capable specialists and veterans. Stats, skills and equipment should feel distinct and valuable, while positioning, teamwork and judgement remain essential. Experience should not make a fighter invulnerable.

4. **Scars tell the story.** Persistent crews accumulate experience, losses, injuries and memorable escapes. Players can carry their progress between battles without needing an organised story or map campaign. Settlement growth remains part of the wider vision; the current test system includes only the approved persistent-play foundations.

5. **Simple rules, complex outcomes.** Reuse clear procedures and concise wording. Depth should come from choices and interacting rules, rather than repeated explanations or separate subsystems for every situation. Skills may bend normal limits to create distinctive roles and combinations.

## Signature experiences

- **The board creates opportunities.** Flanking, changing a route, operating machinery or manipulating cover can be as useful as a direct attack.
- **Choices involve competing risks.** Pursuing an objective, rescuing a casualty, collecting loot or withdrawing can each mean giving up another opportunity.
- **Both players stay involved.** Alternating unit activations, ALERT and reactions create decisions about timing and exposure.
- **Battles create stories.** Desperate rescues, clever terrain use and unexpected outcomes give crews an identity; persistent play carries those stories forward.

## Design boundaries

- Keep the focus on small crews and decisions made on the battlefield. Roster choices matter, but should not decide the outcome before play begins.
- Give missions meaningful routes to success without killing. Casualties can help secure objectives without becoming the default scoring system.
- Make movement and interaction worth choosing. Holding a position can be useful; standing still and trading shots should not dominate every battle.
- Keep the game playable with suitable models, physical references and markers. Digital tools can assist without becoming necessary to resolve a battle.
- Support varied crews and emergent stories while pursuing fair choices and useful counterplay. Narrative variety is not a reason to leave a dominant strategy unchecked.
