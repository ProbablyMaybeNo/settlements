==============================================================================
SHOULD STRESS SURVIVE BEING DOWNED?   exact + seeded MC
==============================================================================

[A] WHAT STRESS DOES A DOWNED MODEL CARRY, FROM THE BURST ALONE?
    Conditional on the target going Down. DEX+2, weapon +2, open
    unarmoured, surplus injury passes converting to Stress.
 dice      S=0      S=1      S=2     S=3+    mean  P(S>=2 | Down)
    1   100.0%     0.0%     0.0%     0.0%    0.00            0.0%
    2    48.8%    51.2%     0.0%     0.0%    0.51            0.0%
    3    23.4%    49.2%    27.4%     0.0%    1.04           27.4%

    A 3-die burst leaves its victim on 1.04 Stress on average,
    and at 2+ Stress - Break-test range -  27.4% of the time.
    A single shot leaves 0.00: a lone bullet that Downs you
    carries no Stress at all, because its one pass IS the wound. So this
    Stress is entirely an Attack Dice phenomenon at the moment of Down.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[B] BUT MOST OF THE STRESS IS NOT FROM THE BURST
    A model usually arrives at Down having already been shot at for
    rounds. Seeded MC, N=20,000: a DEX+2 rifleman is fired on until Down
    by single-shot weapons only - NO Attack Dice anywhere.
     S=0      S=1      S=2      S=3     S=4+    mean   P(S>=2)
   59.6%    24.3%     9.6%     4.0%     2.5%    0.67     16.1%

    With NO Attack Dice in the game at all, a model reaches Down carrying
    0.67 Stress on average and is in Break-test range  16.1% of the time.
    Against 1.04 /  27.4% from a 3-die burst in one Action.

    >> THE RULING IS NOT ABOUT ATTACK DICE. Ordinary single-shot fire
       already puts more Stress on a downed model than a burst does,
       because it takes several attempts and every failed one Pins.
       Attack Dice compress that into one Action; they did not create it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[B2] BOTH RULES TOGETHER - the case actually on the table
     Same MC, but the shooter carries a 3-die weapon and surplus injury
     passes convert to Stress. This is what Stress-at-Down looks like if
     BOTH open items are ruled yes.
                     configuration    mean   P(S>=2)   P(S>=4)
      1 die, no conversion (today)    0.66     15.5%      2.5%
             3 dice, no conversion    0.97     22.7%      3.6%
          3 dice + surplus->Stress    1.44     40.8%      5.6%

     Adopting both roughly 2.2x the Stress a downed model
     carries (0.66 -> 1.44), and takes P(in Break-test range)
     from  15.5% to  40.8%. The BugOut band (4+) goes
       2.5% ->   5.6%.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[C] THE SHARP END - what happens when someone revives him?
    A Stabilised fighter opens his next activation with a Break test.
 Stress   test?  NRV+1 fail  NRV+2 fail  NRV+3 fail      failure means
      0      no        0.0%        0.0%        0.0%            no test
      1      no        0.0%        0.0%        0.0%            no test
      2     yes       60.0%       50.0%       40.0%               Bolt
      3     yes       70.0%       60.0%       50.0%             Broken
      4     yes       80.0%       70.0%       60.0%   BUGOUT (removed)
      5     yes       90.0%       80.0%       70.0%   BUGOUT (removed)

    The 4+ row is the one that matters. A crew spends an Action AND an
    INT test to Stabilise a friend; he stands up, rolls, and on a failure
    at 4+ Stress he BUGS OUT - removed from play. The rescue bought
    nothing, and it cost a whole activation to buy it.

    How often does that actually happen, per revival, at NRV +2?
                        game state  fails 1st test   BUGS OUT  ~1 in N bug out
      1 die, no conversion (today)            8.8%       1.9%          1 in 53
             3 dice, no conversion           12.8%       2.7%          1 in 37
          3 dice + surplus->Stress           22.5%       4.2%          1 in 24

    TODAY'S GAME, if Stress simply persists:   8.8% of revived
    fighters lose the activation,   1.9% walk off - about 1 in 53.
    WITH BOTH ATTACK DICE RULES:  22.5% and   4.2% - about 1 in 24.

    Both land in story territory rather than feels-bad territory. The
    second is the one to watch: a fifth of revivals wasted is a lot more
    noticeable than a tenth, and it is the version that also has three
    dice hitting the model on the way down.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[D] THREE WAYS TO RULE IT, and what each costs

    1. STRESS PERSISTS IN FULL  (Ross's reading)
       Revived fighter tests immediately, can BugOut.
       + Realistic, dramatic, zero new rules text - it is the ABSENCE of a
         clearing rule, so it is literally the cheapest option to write.
       + Makes Stabilise a real decision instead of an automatic yes.
       - The double-punish above: 22.5% of revivals fail at once, 4.2% are
         removed outright after someone spent an Action saving them.
       - Interacts with the death-spiral risk already flagged in
         `Dice Mechanic - Sim Findings` 7.
       - Note those figures are for BOTH Attack Dice rules adopted. On
         today's game the same rule costs only 8.8% / 1.9%.

    2. STABILISE CLEARS TO 1 STRESS  (the valve)
       "A Stabilised fighter returns Shaken, however much Stress it had."
       + One clause. Keeps the flavour - he comes back rattled, at -1.
       + Removes the bad beat entirely; no test on revival, ever.
       - Loses exactly the beat Ross wants: no nerve test on standing up.

    3. PERSISTS, BUT NO BUGOUT ON THE FIRST TEST  (capped)
       Failure on the revival test caps at Broken - lose the activation.
       + Keeps the test and the drama, removes only the walk-off-the-table.
       - A special case, and special cases are what this project cuts.
