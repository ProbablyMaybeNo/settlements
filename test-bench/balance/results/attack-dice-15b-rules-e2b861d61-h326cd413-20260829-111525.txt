    Pins on a downed target, 'stop the sequence' and 'resolve everything'
    describe the same surviving-target outcomes. Only R3 differs.

    The brief's line - 'a 3-die weapon that whiffs every wound still piles
    3 Stress in one Action' - is FALSE under R3 and true under R2/R4.

    3    resolve_all      1.080                4.7%
    3      pick+pins      0.738                0.0%

    R3 and R4 cap the Action at ONE wound however many dice were rolled,
    so a 3-die weapon can NEVER take a WND-2 or WND-3 target from full to
    Down -   0.0% and   0.0%, exactly zero by construction.
    R1/R2 kill a WND-2 outright  29.5% of the time and a WND-3   4.7%,
    averaging 1.080 wounds against 0.738.

    This is the sharpest difference between the two rules and it is an
    IDENTITY choice, not a balance one:
      R3/R4 -> Attack Dice SHRED RANK-AND-FILE, DO NOT MELT CHARACTERS.
               A leader is a hard target no burst can shortcut.
      R1/R2 -> Attack Dice are the ANTI-CHARACTER tool - the more WND a
               target has, the better multiple dice get relative to one.
    Note WND 2-3 is not hypothetical: it is the campaign veteran ceiling
    (Level 7, +Tough), so this rules on how durable a grown leader feels.

[D] IS 'THE SHOOTER CHOOSES' A REAL CHOICE?
    Down vs Pinned, from the rules as written:
      Can still Shoot / Interact?    Down: no                           Pinned: YES
      Can be cleared by the target?  Down: no, needs Stabilize          Pinned: yes, spend Move
      Costs the enemy an Action?     Down: yes, Stabilize or bleed out  Pinned: no
      Removes the model from play?   Down: yes if not stabilised        Pinned: no
      Feeds the Stress ladder?       Down: n/a                          Pinned: +1 Stress

    Down strictly dominates Pinned on every axis that matters at WND 1.
    So 'the shooter chooses' offers a choice that has exactly one correct
    answer, every single time. That is a decision point that costs table
    time and returns no agency - the thing Out of Scope's anti-bloat
    tenet exists to catch.

    IT BECOMES A REAL CHOICE IN EXACTLY TWO CASES:
      1. PAYLOAD WEAPONS. A payload lands in place of Pinned, so the pick
         is 'wound' vs 'Bleeding/Fire/Suppressive'. Against a WND 2-3
         target, Bleeding (a two-round death clock) can beat 1 of 3 WND.
      2. MULTI-WOUND TARGETS, for the same reason.
    Both are the same condition: the choice is real only when one wound
    is NOT decisive. At WND 1 with a vanilla weapon it never is.

[E] THE STAT-CEILING PROBLEM - true under EVERY rule
    P(wound) per Action vs a maxed-out shooter on one die.
    A DEX +6 marksman - the hard stat cap - with a medium weapon reaches
     54.0% wound/Action. It cannot go higher; the natural-1 rule caps
    to-hit at 90%.
                           shooter  wound/Action    vs cap
   DEX +6 marksman, AD 1 (the cap)         54.0%      +0%
              DEX +2 Fighter, AD 2         59.0%      +9%
              DEX +2 Fighter, AD 3         73.8%     +37%
              DEX +0 Recruit, AD 3         56.1%      +4%
                      DEX -1, AD 3         44.9%     -17%

    ATTACK DICE BREAK THE STAT CEILING. Two dice on an ordinary Fighter
    beat the best marksman the game permits, and a DEX +0 Recruit with
    three dice is within a whisker of one. No amount of pricing fixes
    that - it is a statement about what skill is worth. If a 25 Cr
    upgrade outperforms four stat points on the hard cap, DEX stops
    being the thing that decides whether you land it (Weapons, tenet 1).
    -> This is the strongest argument for RARITY/RANK GATING over price.

[F] FAIR PRICE UNDER EACH RULE
    Catalogue rate: 10 Cr per +1 Damage = 167 Cr per 1.0 wounds/Action.
          rule   AD2 cum   AD3 cum   draft 25/60                    verdict
  stop_on_down       38        63        25 / 60         draft UNDER by +5%
      pick_one       38        63        25 / 60         draft UNDER by +5%
   resolve_all       38        63        25 / 60         draft UNDER by +5%
     pick+pins       38        63        25 / 60         draft UNDER by +5%

    ALL FOUR RULES PRICE IDENTICALLY (38 / 63 cumulative), because they
    deliver identical WOUNDS and the catalogue's exchange rate prices
    wounds only. The rule choice is free at the till; it is paid for in
    suppression [B] and in character durability [C], neither of which the
    Damage ladder has ever priced.

    AGAINST THE DRAFT: AD 3 at +60 is right - fair is 63, a -5% gap.
    AD 2 at +25 is the underpriced rung - fair is 38, a -35% gap.
    The draft's step runs the wrong way: it should be roughly +40 then
    +25, not +25 then +35. Die 2 is the expensive one.

    THIS SUPERSEDES the '+25/+60 is about half price' line in the first
    pass. That figure came from resolve_all's UNCAPPED E[wounds] (60/120),
    which counts overkill on a WND-1 target as value. It is not value.
    Capping at the target's actual WND - which every rule here does - the
    honest fair price is 38/63 and the draft is close on AD 3.

    The auto-include finding is UNAFFECTED and still stands: it compares
    Attack Dice against BODIES, not against the Damage ladder. Both are
    true at once - the price matches the damage-step rate, and the damage
    ladder is itself cheap relative to a 100 Cr Fighter.
