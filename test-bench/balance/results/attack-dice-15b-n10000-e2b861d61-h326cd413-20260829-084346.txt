   heavy |  0.240  0.480  0.720 |  0.240  0.442  0.611 |         12.7%
  hidden |  0.180  0.360  0.540 |  0.180  0.338  0.478 |          7.8%

    The suppression identity is REAL, and it peaks where the target is
    ARMOURED BUT EXPOSED - open cover, heavy armour (0.900 resolve_all).
    Stress is p_hit x (1 - p_wound), so cover suppresses it and armour
    feeds it. Cover cuts BOTH wounds and Stress; armour converts wounds
    INTO Stress. A 3-die weapon into a dug-in target is simply weak; into
    an armoured target in the open it is a suppression engine.
    Note the Break column: one 3-die Action puts an armoured open target
    into Break-test range 16.2% of the time on its own, from a standing
    start of zero Stress. That is a real second identity, not a consolation.

[E] TEST 4 - ACTIVATIONS-TO-DOWN, POINTS-NORMALIZED
    Live prices out of points/ticks.py, not the draft's assumptions.

    Standard Ranged +2 @18"  = 15 Cr
    Sidearm +2 @8"           = 15 Cr
    Fighter body             = 100 Cr    Recruit body = 70 Cr
    Draft Attack Dice adder  = +25 (AD2) / +60 (AD3) cumulative

    >> A second attack die (+25 Cr) costs MORE THAN THE WHOLE RIFLE
       (15 Cr) it is bolted to. AD 3 (+60) is 4x the weapon and
       60% of the Fighter carrying it.

    Target: standard body in LIGHT cover / LIGHT armour (the modal cell).
    E[activations to Down] = 1 / P(Down per activation).
                             configuration    Cr  dice/act  P(Down)  E[acts]   Cr*acts
                     1x Fighter, rifle AD1   115         1    25.0%     4.00       460
                     1x Fighter, rifle AD2   140         2    43.8%     2.29       320
                     1x Fighter, rifle AD3   175         3    57.8%     1.73       303
            1x Fighter, rifle +4 dmg (AD1)   135         1    35.0%     2.86       386
                  2x Recruit, pistol (AD1)   170         2    43.8%     2.29       389
1x Fighter rifle + 1x Recruit pistol (AD1)   200         2    43.8%     2.29       457

    Lower Cr*acts is better.
      BEST : 1x Fighter, rifle AD3  (303)
      WORST: 1x Fighter, rifle AD1  (460)

    VERDICT ON THE COSTING TABLE: the stepped price DOES NOT close the
    tempo gap. AD 3 at full draft cost is the most Credit-efficient way to
    put a body down on this board, and AD 1 is the least. Every Attack
    Dice rung beats every alternative use of the same Credits, including
    the two-cheap-bodies build the mechanic is supposed to lose to.

    THE REASON IS THE DENOMINATOR. Attack Dice are bought per WEAPON but
    they multiply the output of a BODY, and the body is the expensive
    part - a Fighter is 100 Cr against a 15 Cr rifle. Buying a
    second die costs 25 Cr; buying a second body to fire a second die
    costs 85 Cr. Any per-die price below the price of a body will
    make dice the efficient purchase, and 25 is far below it.

    Two cheap bodies still bring a second activation, a second objective-
    carrier and a second body to lose - real value this test does not
    price, because it measures shooting only. That is the honest caveat,
    and it does not close a 1.5x Credit-efficiency gap.

[F] TEST 5 - IS AD 3 AN AUTO-INCLUDE (the Heavy Gunner test)?
    Findings takeaway #6 flagged Heavy Gunner at 68.9% avg win rate and
    said to watch it. Same treatment here.

    Cost is FIELDED cost - body + weapon - not weapon alone. A weapon-only
    denominator flatters cheap weapons on expensive bodies and is what
    makes Attack Dice look expensive when they are not; the die multiplies
    the body's output, so the body belongs in the price of the output.
                            fielded purchase    Cr  wounds/act   per 100 Cr
          Fighter + Std Ranged +2 (baseline)   115       0.360        0.313
                     Fighter + Std Ranged +3   125       0.420        0.336
                     Fighter + Std Ranged +4   135       0.480        0.356
 Specialist + Heavy Ranged +3 (Heavy Gunner)   175       0.490        0.280
       Fighter + rifle AD2 (draft +25, stop)   140       0.590        0.422
       Fighter + rifle AD3 (draft +60, stop)   175       0.738        0.422
        Fighter + rifle AD2 (draft +25, all)   140       0.720        0.514
        Fighter + rifle AD3 (draft +60, all)   175       1.080        0.617

    Baseline Fighter+rifle: 0.313 wounds/act per 100 Cr.
    Heavy Gunner benchmark: 0.280 per 100 Cr at 175 Cr fielded.
    Fighter + AD3 rifle   : 0.422 per 100 Cr at 175 Cr fielded.

    AD 3 IS AN AUTO-INCLUDE AT THE DRAFT PRICE. FLAG RAISED.
    Every Attack Dice rung outperforms every Damage step per Credit, and
    AD 3 beats the Heavy Gunner benchmark by +51% on the SAME fielded
    Credits (175 vs 175) - and that is the pessimistic
    stop_on_down reading. Under resolve_all it is worse again.
    Findings takeaway #6 asked for price-plus-gate on Heavy Gunner. This
    wants the same: the derived price in [G], AND a rarity/rank gate.

[G] WHAT SHOULD IT COST? - derived from the shipped catalogue, not asserted
    The catalogue's own offensive exchange rate is CREDITS_DAMAGE = 10 Cr per +1 Damage.
    +1 Damage (+2 to +3) on the modal open target buys +0.0600 wounds/Action
    -> the shipped rate is 167 Cr per 1.0 wounds/Action.

    priced under resolve_all:
            d wounds/act   fair Cr  cumulative   draft  draft/fair
     die 2        0.3600      60.0        60.0      25       0.42x
     die 3        0.3600      60.0       120.0      60       0.50x

    priced under stop_on_down:
            d wounds/act   fair Cr  cumulative   draft  draft/fair
     die 2        0.2304      38.4        38.4      25       0.65x
     die 3        0.1475      24.6        63.0      60       0.95x

    Both readings agree in direction: the draft is UNDERPRICED. +25/+60 is
    about half the output-equivalent price under resolve_all and about
    two thirds of it under stop_on_down. The draft's own instinct - that a
    flat per-die price 'would underprice die 2' - was right in direction
    and much too small in magnitude.

    They DISAGREE on the shape, and the disagreement is the whole ruling:
      resolve_all  -> dice are LINEAR (60 / 60). A flat per-die price is
                      correct and the stepped table is the wrong shape.
      stop_on_down -> dice DECAY (38 / 25). Die 3 is worth less than die 2,
                      so the step should run DOWNWARD, not upward.
    The draft's stepped-UPWARD table (+25 then +35) is the one shape that
    is wrong under BOTH readings. That is the finding, and it does not
    depend on which way the WND-1 ruling goes.

    RECOMMENDED, pending that ruling: AD2 +40, AD3 +65 cumulative under
    stop_on_down; AD2 +60, AD3 +120 under resolve_all. Both leave AD 3
    below the Heavy Gunner's Cr-efficiency instead of above it.
